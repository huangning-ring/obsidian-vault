## PPT 2: Introduction to Mycoplasma Pneumonia
**Mycoplasma pneumoniae**
- Smallest self-replicating bacteria
- No cell wall (unique structure)
- Causes "walking pneumonia"
- Common in children and young adults
**Clinical Features**
- Gradual onset of symptoms
- Persistent dry cough
- Low-grade fever
- Headache and fatigue
***
## PPT 3: Why Penicillin is Ineffective
**Penicillin Mechanism**
- Targets bacterial cell wall synthesis
- Inhibits peptidoglycan formation
- Causes bacterial cell death
**Mycoplasma's Defense**
- Lacks cell wall completely
- Has triple-layered membrane with sterols
- Naturally resistant to beta-lactam antibiotics
---
## PPT 4: Effective Antibiotics - Macrolides
**First-Line Treatment**
- Azithromycin
- Clarithromycin
- Erythromycin
**Mechanism of Action**
- Binds to 50S ribosomal subunit
- Inhibits protein synthesis
- Bacteristatic effect
---
## PPT 5: Alternative Antibiotics
**Second-Line Options**
- Doxycycline (tetracyclines)
- Levofloxacin (fluoroquinolones)
- Moxifloxacin
---
## PPT 6: Adult Side Effects and Prevention
**Common Side Effects**
- Gastrointestinal: nausea, diarrhea
- Headache
- Abnormal taste
**Prevention Strategies**
- Take with food to reduce gastrointestinal upset
- Monitor liver function
- Avoid grapefruit
---
## PPT 7: Pediatric Considerations
**Children's Side Effects**
- Diarrhea and vomit
- Abdominal pain
- Headache
**Special Precautions**
- Monitor for prolonged QT interval
- Avoid in children with heart conditions
- Complete full course of treatment
---
## PPT 8: Monitoring and Management
**Clinical Monitoring**
- Watch for persistent symptoms
- Check for antibiotic resistance
- Monitor liver enzymes
**When to Change Treatment**
- No improvement after 72 hours
- Worsening symptoms
- Development of complications
---
## PPT 9: Prevention of Spread
**Infection Control**
- Cover coughs and sneezes
- Frequent hand washing
- Avoid close contact when sick
**High-Risk Groups**
- Young children
- Elderly
- Immunocompromised ones
---
## PPT 10: Summary and Key Points
**Key Takeaways**
- Penicillin ineffective due to no cell wall
- Macrolides are first-line treatment
- Monitor for side effects in adults and children
- Complete full antibiotic course
**Recovery Time**
- 1-4 weeks with treatment
- Cough may persist longer
- Rest and hydration important

**Slide 2: Introduction**
Mycoplasma is a very small bacteria that causes respiratory infections. It's special because it doesn't have a cell wall like other bacteria. This makes it resistant to some antibiotics. It often causes "walking pneumonia" because people can still walk around even when sick. Symptoms start slowly with cough, fever, and tiredness. It's common in children.
Here shows some common symptoms of Mycoplasma infections.
**Slide 3: Penicillin Problem**
Penicillin works by attacking the cell wall of bacteria. But Mycoplasma doesn't have a cell wall, so penicillin can't hurt it. Instead, Mycoplasma has a strong membrane that protects it. This is why doctors don't use penicillin for this infection.
**Slide 4: Macrolide Treatment**
Macrolide antibiotics like azithromycin are the best choice. Usually used as the first-line treatment. They work by stopping the bacteria from making proteins it needs to grow. This slows down the infection and helps your body fight it off. These medicines are usually taken for 5-10 days.
**Slide 6: Adult Side Effects**
Adults taking these medicines might feel sick to their stomach, have nausea, diarrhea, or get headaches. To feel better, take the medicine with food. Tell your doctor if you have liver problems or heart rhythm issues. Don't eat grapefruit while taking these medicines.
Doctors will check if the medicine is working after a few days. If you're not getting better, they might change the medicine.
**Slide 9: Stopping Spread**
This infection spreads through coughs and sneezes. You should cover your mouth when you cough. And wash your hands often. Stay home from school or work when you're sick. Young children and older people are more likely to get very sick, so you should pay more attention to the elderly's health if there is in your family.
**Slide 10: Summary**
Remember: penicillin doesn't work for this infection. Macrolides are the first choice. Watch for side effects and finish all your medicine. It takes time to get better completely. Rest and drink plenty of fluids to help your body heal completely.
