Bat is considered a natural reservoir of various important pathogens, including severe acute
respiratory syndrome coronavirus (SARS-CoV), SARS-CoV-2, Ebola virus, and Nipah virus. To study
these viruses’ pathogenicity and proliferation efficacy and viral tolerance mechanisms in bats, bat-
derived cell lines, and primary cultured cells are used. However, these do not adequately reflect
the exact biology of bats, and establishing new bat-related research models is necessary. Organoid
culture can recapitulate organ structure, functions, and diseases. The respiratory tract is one of the
primary routes of viral infection, and the establishment of bat lung organoids (BLO) is necessary to
study the viral susceptibility in bats. Therefore, we aimed to establish a culture method of BLO from
Rousettus leschenaultia that died of natural causes. The generated BLO successfully recapitulated the
characteristics of pulmonary epithelial structure and morphology. BLO expressed the entry receptors
for coronavirus, Angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (ACE2), and Transmembrane Protease Serine 2
(TMPRSS2), and alveolar type 2 cells were successfully sorted from BLO, which has an important role
for the development of viral infection in the respiratory system. Furthermore, we showed that BLO had
no susceptibility to Pteropine orthoreovirus (PRV) compared to bat intestinal organoids. Collectively,
our established bat organoid culture models including this BLO might become promising in vitro
biomaterials to study the biology of bat-derived infectious diseases.
Keywords Bat, Rousettus leschenaultii, Lung organoid, ACE2, TMPRSS2, Alveolar type 2 cells
Bat is known as the most important natural reservoir for various emerging viruses including Hendra virus,
Ebola virus, Nipah virus, and Marburg virus, which induce severe illness in humans and animals1. Especially
coronavirus, severe acute respiratory syndrome (SARS) coronavirus, which is the causative agent of SARS
occurred in southern China2, Middle-East respiratory syndrome (MERS) coronavirus, which causes MERS
reported in the first patient in Saudi Arabia, and SARS-CoV-2, which causes coronavirus disease 2019
(COVID-19). Like coronaviruses, Pteropine orthoreovirus (PRV) and influenza A virus (IAV) are known to
cause respiratory illness in humans and have been reported isolated from various species of bats, including
Roussette’s bat3–7. However, there is no report that these pathogens induce mass mortality or shortening of the
life span of bats, and no clear clinical symptoms have been confirmed in the experimental infections8.

The immune system of bats has been studied, and various related factors and species specificities are being
revealed9. Type I interferon (IFN) expression was rapidly induced under viral infection10. The bat-specific
variation and mutation on innate immunity-related genes including IFN and major histocompatibility complex
(MHC) class I, and novel antiviral mechanism via IFN-ω were detected11. Additionally, IFN-stimulated factors,
such as Mx protein which is upregulated in response to viral infection are essential to control virus replication in
bat cells12. For the elucidation of virus tolerance mechanism in bats, not only in vivo assay but also bat primary
cultured cells and immortalized cell lines are established as experimental tools13. However, these new findings
did not explain the reason that bats are susceptible to virus replication although they have little symptom under
these virus infections which show fatal symptoms in humans and other animals. Therefore, the development of
new in vitro models that can represent native bat cells to study bat-related viruses will be promising.
Organoids are recently developed 3D complex in vitro structures generated mainly from stem cells in the
tissues and typically recapitulate the original architecture and most characteristics of their primary tissues, such
as self-renewal, organization, differentiation, and functions14–17. The last decade has witnessed a revolution in
the establishment of organoids from several organs18–21, which have opened new avenues for studying organ
development, drug testing22,23, drug resistance24–30, precision and translational medicine9, tissue transplantation,
and importantly disease modeling31–33.
Since coronavirus including SARS-CoV and SARS-CoV-2 are detected in fecal samples and rectal swab
samples from bats, such as Horseshoe bats, the first bat intestinal organoids were established as an in vitro model
for evaluation of susceptibility to pathogens causing respiratory disease, including SARS-CoV-2 and PRV34.
However, few reports have established bat lung organoids and evaluated their susceptibility to these viruses35,36.
We, therefore, aimed to establish and characterize a bat lung organoid (BLO) culture model from Rousettus
leschenaultia that could recapitulate the characteristics of bat lung tissues.
Materials and methods
Chemicals and reagents
To generate BLO, the minced tissues were digested with advanced Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium (DMEM)
(Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA) including Liberase TH (Roche Diagnostics Gmbh,
Mannheim, Germany) at a concentration of 0.125 mg/mL media according to the manufacturerʼs instructions.
The cells were seeded in Matrigel (BD Bioscience, San Jose, CA, USA) and cultured with a stem cell-boosting
medium. Antibody sources were as follows: E-cadherin (R&D System, Minneapolis, MN, USA); Mucin 5AC
(MUC5AC); Transmembrane Protease Serine 2 (TMPRSS2); Secretoglobin Family 1 A Member 1 (SCGB1A1)
(Bioss, Woburn, MA, USA); CK5; Angiotensin-converting enzyme II (ACE2); Surfactant-associated protein C
(SFTPC) (Affinity Biosciences LTD, OH, USA). Fluorescent secondary antibodies were as follows: Alexa Fluor
488 goat anti-rabbit IgG; Alexa Fluor 48 goat anti-mouse IgG (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc.).
Preparation of bat tissues
Between October 2019 to March 2020, lung tissues of six adult Leschenault’s rousette (Rousettus leschenaultii)
that died of natural causes were isolated during the pathological dissection and provided kindly from Ueno
Zoo (Tokyo, Japan). Age of these bats was difficult to estimate. The isolated tissues were placed in a precooled
preservation buffer (see details in Table 1) and transported in a cool condition to the laboratory.
Establishment of BLO culture method
The generation of BLO was carried out as described before37. After collecting lung tissues from 6 bats, they were
washed three times with cold phosphate buffer saline (PBS), chopped properly into fine pieces, and transferred
into tubes containing advanced DMEM media with 0.125 mg/mL Liberase TH for digestion and isolation of
lung epithelial cells. The tubes were then incubated at 37 °C in a shaking incubator for 30 min and pipetted every
15 min. The resultant cell suspension was then passed into a 70 μm nylon net cell strainer and spun down at
600 g for 5 min at 4 °C. The cell pellets were then washed three times with PBS and suspended in Matrigel on
ice and dispersed in a 24-well plate (40 µL/well). After the Matrigel was polymerized to form a droplet in the
CO incubator at 37 °C for 30 min, 500 µl of stem cell-stimulating medium (see details in Table 2) was added to
2
each well. The media were changed 2–3 times weekly. Organoids were passaged every 7–14 days by using 5 mM
EDTA/PBS solution at 1:2–4 split as described before16. Photomicrographs of the organoids were acquired using
an Olympus CKX-31 inverted microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan).

Media component analysis
To search for the suitable culture condition of BLO, we further investigated the effects of several supplements on
the growth, proliferation rate, morphology, and maintenance of the organoids as described before37. The obtained
BLO were trypsinized for 5 min and strained by a 70 μm cell strainer (Falcon, Cary, NC, USA). Thereafter, 1 × 103
organoid cells were seeded into a 10 µL Matrigel on 96-well culture plates and incubated in the CO incubator
2
at 37 °C for 20 min. After polymerization of the Matrigel, the organoid cells were cultured in the base medium
(see details in Table 3) either alone or plus L-WNR condition medium (1:1 v/v) or human epidermal growth
factor (EGF, 50 ng/mL, PeproTech, Inc.), or fibroblast growth factor-2 (FGF2, 20 ng/mL, PeproTech, Inc.), or
FGF7 (5 ng/mL, PeproTech, Inc.), or FGF10 (20 ng/mL, PeproTech, Inc.), or insulin-like growth factor (IGF,
10 ng/mL, PeproTech, Inc.), or transforming growth factor-alpha (TGF-α) (20 ng/mL, PeproTech, Inc.). The
culture medium was changed every two days. Quantification of the effects on the growth and proliferation of
organoids was assessed on day seven after culture using the PrestoBlue assay kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific)
and a microplate reader (TECAN, Seestrasse, Switzerland). Photomicrographs of the organoids from different
culture media were captured using an Olympus CKX-31 inverted microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan). The
effective components were chosen and used as a culture medium (see details in Table 2) for BLO and applied in
subsequent experiments.
Hematoxylin & Eosin staining
The paraffin-embedded sections from BLO and the original tissues were prepared and stained with Hematoxylin
& Eosin (H&E) as described before32.
Immunofluorescence staining
Frozen sections from BLO and the original tissues were prepared and immunofluorescence staining was
performed as described before 16,32.
Transmission electron microscopy (TEM)
Preparation of organoids for TEM and TEM staining were performed as described before37. After the BLO were
fixed with 2.5% glutaraldehyde for three h at room temperature (RT) in 0.1% cacodylate (pH 7.4), they were

sodium cacodylate (pH 7.4) for 2 h at 4 °C. After washing with distilled water, BLO were dehydrated in a graded
ethanol series (50, 70, 80, 90, 95, 99.5 up to 100%) and embedded in epon. Thereafter, 70–110 nm ultrathin
sections were prepared with a diamond knife on a Leica UC7 ultramicrotome and conveyedonto 50 Mesh copper

Cell sorting and culture of alveolar type 2 cells (AT2 cells)
Since viable AT2 cells were successfully sorted based on their LysoTracker staining characteristics in the previous
report38, we tried to sort AT2 cells from BLO using the LysoTracker. After dissociation of organoids with TrypLE
and neutralizing it with a 10% volume of fetal bovine serum (FBS; Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc.), the cells were
incubated with media including 10 nM LysoTracker green DND-26 (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc.) at 37 °C for
30 min. Thereafter, they were filtered through a 70 μm nylon net cell strainer. After that, they were washed and
resuspended in organoid media. After LysoTracker cells were sorted, they were embedded into Matrigel and
cultured with organoid media including 10 µM Y-27,632 for 24 h, followed by regular organoid media. Cell
sorting of BLO was carried out by an On-chip Sort (On-chip Biotechnologies Co., Ltd, Tokyo, Japan).
Susceptibility of BLO to Pteropine Orthoreovirus
Pteropine Orthoreovirus (Samal-24 strain (PRV)) was kindly supplied by Dr. Satoshi Taniguchi, NIID, Japan.
The virus susceptibility of BLO to PRV was evaluated, and compared with that of bat intestinal organoids (BIO)
which were established by our laboratory in a previous study37. PRV was amplified with Vero cells and the
supernatant was collected 72 h post-inoculation (hpi). The supernatant was centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 15 min
and stored at -80 °C until use. The BLO and BIO were infected with PRV to while maintaining the steric structure
of the organoids as described before37. In brief, the cultured organoids were washed with PBS and 500 µl of 5
mM EDTA/PBS was added to each well and incubated on ice for 90 min. The cultured organoids were collected
by centrifugation and washed with PBS three times, and then the organoids were incubated in a culture medium
with PRV at MOI = 0.01 at 37 °C for 1 h. Subsequently, the organoids were gently washed with PBS and cultured
in Matrigel as described above. The supernatant medium, Matrigel, and the organoids were collected 2, 24, 48,
and 72 hpi and stored at -80 °C until analysis. After collecting the supernatant, 360 µl of 5 mM EDTA/PBS was
added per well and incubated on ice for 90 min., and centrifuged. The levels of PRV genome in BLO and BIO
were evaluated with real-time PCR assay39. Additionally, the cell viability of BLO and BIO infected with PRV
was compared to mock infection using Presto Blue assay kit at 24, 48, and 72 hpi as mentioned before in the
previous study37.
Statistical analysis
Data are expressed as means ± SEM. Statistical significance was evaluated using a one-way analysis of variance
(ANOVA) followed by Bonferroni’s test. Values of P < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.
Results
Generation of primary BLO
To generate BLO, lung tissues from 6 bats that died of natural causes at the zoo were collected and processed.
Then, the generated organoids were characterized (Fig. 1A). As shown in Fig. 1B, the BLO were successfully
generated, passaged, and mimicked the multicellular structure of the bat lung epithelium. Further, the BLO
recapitulated the histology of the original tissues (Fig. 1C). Using TEM, we observed that the ultrastructural
morphology of BLO also mimicked that of lung tissues (Fig. 1D). In BLO, the organoids consisted of a polarized,
pseudostratified airway epithelium containing basal, secretory, and microvilli cells (Fig. 1D).
Effects of different media supplements on the growth of BLO
Next, we searched for the suitable media supplements for the optimal growth of BLO. Using several supplements
(Wnt 3a, Noggin, R-spondin (WNR), EGF, FGF2, FGF7, FGF10, IGF, or TGF-α), we seeded the same number of
organoid cells and checked their effects on the growth and proliferation rate of BLO (Fig. 2A). In BLO, TGF-α,
EGF, WNR, and FGF7 had a significant effect on their growth and proliferation rate and the other supplements
showed no significant effects compared with control (base medium) (Fig. 2B, C). According to these findings,
we identified the essential supplements for BLO (WNR + EGF + FGF7 + TGF-α) and cultured them in these
components including base medium.
Cellular components of BLO
To confirm the cellular components of BLO, we checked the expression of several specific markers of lung tissues
using an immunofluorescence staining. Expression of an epithelial cell marker, E-cadherin, was confirmed in
BLO, as well as in the original tissues (Fig. 3A). In BLO and bat lung tissues, expressions of a respiratory goblet
cell marker, MUC5AC, airway basal cell marker, CK5, and a pulmonary cell marker, surfactant-associated
protein C (SFTPC) were observed, while expression of a Clara cell marker, SCGB1A1 was not confirmed in
BLO (Fig. 3A). These data indicate that BLO recapitulated the majority of the cellular components of bat lung
tissue. It has been confirmed that the new coronavirus uses the ACE2 receptor to enter the cells40 with the aid
of the transmembrane cellular protease, TMPRSS241. Based on these data, we analyzed the expression levels of
ACE2 and TMPRSS2 in BLO. ACE2 was clearly expressed on the apical surface or basal membrane of BLO and
the alveolar cells of the lung tissues (Fig. 3B). TMPRSS2 was expressed not only at the basal surface but also at
several sites of BLO and the original tissues (Fig. 3B). These data suggest the potential of this model to analyze
SARS-CoV-2 infection in the lung.

Isolation of alveolar type 2 cells (AT2 cells)
AT2 cells are responsible for the production and secretion of lung surfactants and the maintenance of alveolar
homeostasis, such as the prevention of virus infection42. In Fig. 3A, we confirmed that BLO consisted of not only
AT2 cells (SFTPC-positive cells) but also airway basal cells (CK5-positive cells). For isolation of pure AT2 cells,
we sorted BLO cells by an AT2 cell marker, Lysotracker (Fig. 4A). First, the affinity of cells to Lysotracker was
confirmed (Fig. 4B). Next, the FACS sorting was performed. The plots showed the isolated AT2 cells based on
the high expression population of Lysotracker staining (Fig. 4C). The non-sorted and sorted organoid cells were
cultured again and the sorted organoids showed a typical luminal shape (Fig. 4D). We confirmed that the ratio
of luminal-type organoids in sorted BLO significantly increased compared with non-sorted BLO (Fig. 4E). These
data suggest the usefulness of our model for studying the direct effects of disease on AT2 cells.
Susceptibility of BLO to PRV
Finally, we performed viral infection experiments using BLO. However, in BLO, PRV genomes were below
detection limits in the supernatant throughout the experiment. Additionally, the genomes were not detected
even in organoids (data not shown). On the other hand, the levels of the virus genome in the supernatant and
Matrigel of BIO peaked at 48 hpi were 2.1 × 108 copies/mL and 1.7 × 106 copies/mL, respectively (Fig. 5).
The cell viability of the organoids infected with PRV was compared to mock infection (Fig. 6). The trend of
cell viability of BLO infected with PRV was similar to BIO, though viral propagation was very different. The cell
viability of BLO infected with PRV was gradually decreased compared to mock infection and was 76% at 72 hpi.
The cell viability of BIO infected with PRV gradually decreased and was 67% at 72 hpi.
Discussion
Bat is known as a natural reservoir of several emerging viruses, such as the Ebola virus, Marburg virus, Nipah
virus, and SARS-CoV-2. After the outbreak of COVID-19, many investigators have been studying the next virus
outbreak prediction. However, there is not enough information about the whole genome data of viruses, even
coronavirus, to study the prediction with high accuracy. In this study, we established BLO culture model and
showed that it recapitulated the ultrastructure of their original tissues as shown in epithelium including basal,
secretory, brush, and microvilli cells as shown before in human airway organoids43,44. Further, we evaluated
whether our established BLO models could be used for virus isolation and studying pathogenicity of viruses
including coronavirus. In the case of coronavirus including SARS-CoV-2, the virus entry to cells and the
replication capacity of the cells mainly depends on ACE2 and transmembrane cellular protease, TMPRSS234,41.
Based on these data, we analyzed the expression levels of these entry factors in BLO. ACE2 was expressed on the
apical membrane in BLO (Fig. 3B), which corresponded with recent reports of human enteroids34 and airway
organoids45. They were successfully used for the virus isolation and infection model of coronavirus. Consistent
with our data, TMPRSS2 was shown to be expressed at multiple sites in the epithelium of respiratory and
gastrointestinal tracts46. These data suggest that our established BLO might be used as new experimental models
of infection or persistent mechanisms of coronavirus in bats.
The AT2 cells are specialized alveolar epithelial cells responsible for the production and secretion of lung
surfactant and maintenance of alveolar homeostasis and can be transformed into AT1 cells specifically following
injury47. LysoTracker Green DND-26 is a fluorescent dye that stains acidic compartments in living cells and has
a high affinity to accumulate in AT2 cells selectively in lamellar bodies. On this basis, we tried to sort the BLO
cells into basal cells and AT2 cells using LysoTracker green DND-26 staining (Fig. 4A). Individual LysoTracker+
sorted cells were further cultured and progressively expanded mostly as cystic luminal organoids with more
flattened morphologies reminiscent of alveoli (Fig. 4D, E). SARS-CoV-2 can infiltrate the lower compartment of
the respiratory tract, inducing severe respiratory failure and a high death rate34. Since AT2 cells are considered
potential therapeutics for acute lung injury and acute respiratory distress syndrome48, our established BLO
can be used for modeling the respiratory viral infectious diseases transmitted by bats, including SARS-CoV-2
infection.
We evaluated the susceptibility of BLO to Pteropine orthoreovirus (PRV) isolated from Eonycteris spelaea
in the Philippines with MOI = 0.01 in comparison with BIO that were established before by our laboratory37.
Whereas PRV was amplified in BIO efficiently, PRV was not amplified in BLO although the reduction in cell
viability was similar for both BIO and BLO. PRV was detected in both oral swab samples and rectal swab samples
in bats and is the causative agent of respiratory disease in humans5,49. Currently, the organ specificity of PRV in
bats is unknown. Our obtained results in BIO and BLO, however, suggested that PRV favor multiplying in the
intestinal tract rather than in the lungs of bats. It indicates that the production of organoids derived from various
organs and their use in infection experiments will make it possible to conduct research on bats, which are not
laboratory animals.
In conclusion, we generated BLO that recapitulated the multicellular structure of the lung epithelium and
thus might be helpful as durable in vitro models for studying transmissible pathogenic infections through bats.
We expect that BLO become a new experimental platform for further isolation and study of other bat pathogens
of high zoonotic potential.
