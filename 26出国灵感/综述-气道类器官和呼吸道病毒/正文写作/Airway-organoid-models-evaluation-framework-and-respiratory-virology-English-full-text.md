# Abstract
Airway organoids reproduce selected structural and functional properties of airway epithelium in expandable human systems. They offer an experimental option between two-dimensional cell culture, animal models, and native tissue for studies of respiratory viruses. This review begins with the definition, cellular origins, and technical development of organoids, then sets out an evaluation framework based on cell lineages, spatial organisation, polarity, physiological function, maturity, reproducibility, sample representativeness, microenvironmental fit, and expansion capacity. The framework asks whether a model suits a particular virological question rather than ranking all models on one scale. The analysis then draws on 10 studies of SARS-CoV-2 and 10 studies of influenza virus to examine viral entry, cell tropism, barrier injury, donor variation, antiviral screening, and cross-species risk assessment. Current evidence suggests that organoids often add information when a conclusion depends on airway cell composition, tissue polarity, receptor and protease expression, or donor background. For some endpoints involving replication, cytokine responses, and neutralisation, however, organoids and air-liquid interface cultures produce similar results. The value of an airway organoid therefore depends on the research question, the quality of the model, and the evidence that it adds. Most virological material discussed here is limited to bibliographic records and public abstracts, so claims of exclusivity still require full-text verification.
# Basic concepts
Organoids are three-dimensional structures formed in vitro through the self-organisation of stem or progenitor cells. They reproduce selected structural and functional properties of the tissue from which they are derived (Wang et al., 2026; Kim et al., 2020). This definition has four elements: a stem or progenitor cell origin, the capacity for self-organisation, culture in a three-dimensional environment, and selective reconstruction of tissue structure and function. Vazquez-Armendariz and Tata (2023) defined lung organoids as "three-dimensional culture systems established from stem cells that can expand and differentiate into structures recapitulating some of the architecture, biology and function of the organ of origin". An organoid is not a complete organ in miniature. It is a selective representation of particular tissue properties. Kim et al. (2020) likewise identified stem cell origin, self-organisation, and three-dimensional culture as common features, and described how organoids form tissue-like structures by modelling organ development or regeneration.
## Two routes of derivation
Organoids are usually generated through one of two routes: pluripotent stem cells, including PSCs and iPSCs, or tissue stem cells, including TSCs and AdSCs (Wang et al., 2026; Kim et al., 2020).
The PSC route uses staged developmental signals to direct differentiation. It can produce structures containing several lineages, although the cells often remain at a late fetal-like stage and show limited maturation (Wang et al., 2026). The TSC route starts directly from a biopsy of the target tissue. Culture is usually faster and the cells more closely resemble adult tissue, but access to donor material limits this route and the resulting models are often dominated by epithelium (Kim et al., 2020).
Vazquez-Armendariz and Tata (2023) also distinguished adult, fetal, and iPSC sources. Media supplements and co-culture conditions shape the final phenotype, so identical starting material can yield different models under different conditions. iPSCs support scale-up and experimental control of developmental processes, whereas adult-derived models are more likely to retain features associated with donor age and disease (Vazquez-Armendariz and Tata, 2023).
Cell source is therefore a stratification variable, not a direct measure of quality. This is why source category C4 is classified but not scored in the evaluation framework used below.
## Uses and limits of organoids
Organoids occupy a position between two-dimensional cell culture and animal models. Two-dimensional systems are easy to manipulate but have limited tissue relevance. Animal models preserve more of the tissue environment but introduce species differences. Human organoids can be cultured and genetically manipulated in vitro, making them useful for studying human-specific development, metabolism, and drug responses that animals may not reproduce accurately (Kim et al., 2020). Kim et al. (2020) described organoids as a complement to existing models rather than a replacement for them.
Organoids remain reductionist models. Wang et al. (2026) noted that current organoids often lack vasculature, immune cells, stromal cells, nerves, mechanical forces, and interactions between organs. Hughes et al. (2022) likewise argued that organoids do not reproduce complete epithelial-stromal or epithelial-immune interactions, or systemic variables. Conclusions therefore need testing in orthogonal models or independent datasets.
Kim et al. (2020) proposed a principle of "appropriate complexity". Simple and complex organoids answer different questions, and unnecessary co-culture can add poorly controlled variation. Model assessment should ask whether the system is sufficient for the research question rather than favouring complexity for its own sake. This principle also informs the evaluation framework below.
# Development of organoid technology
## Technical origins
Early collagen-gel cultures and Matrigel embedding provided cells with three-dimensional matrices. These systems did not rely primarily on stem cell self-organisation and generally aimed to maintain three-dimensional cell morphology rather than reconstruct tissue architecture (Kim et al., 2020).
In 2009, Sato et al. reported in Nature that a single Lgr5+ adult intestinal stem cell could form a three-dimensional organoid with crypt-villus architecture in the absence of a mesenchymal niche (Sato et al., 2009). This study demonstrated the self-organising capacity of adult tissue stem cells and established the tissue stem cell route to organoid culture (Kim et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2026).
In 2013, Lancaster et al. reported cerebral organoids derived from human pluripotent stem cells in Nature. These organoids self-organised into three-dimensional structures containing several brain regions and were used to model microcephaly (Lancaster et al., 2013). The work showed that PSCs could form complex, multi-region organoids for disease modelling and established the PSC route (Kim et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2026).
The routes involve different compromises. TSCs permit direct expansion of relatively mature cells but depend on tissue access. PSCs model development and generate multiple lineages, although their derivatives often remain immature.
# Development of airway organoids
## Three-dimensional airway epithelial culture before long-term organoid expansion, 2002 to 2016
Researchers placed airway epithelial cells in three-dimensional environments before long-term expandable airway organoids became available. Castillon et al. (2002) cultured human respiratory surface epithelial cells in collagen gels and observed polarised CFTR expression. Castillon et al. (2004) then showed that spheroid cells could reconstruct a differentiated airway surface epithelium. Deslee et al. (2007) developed a bronchial epithelial spheroid model, while Kaisani et al. (2014) observed branching morphology in immortalised bronchial epithelial cells grown in three dimensions. These studies produced three-dimensional differentiation of airway epithelium, but they did not yet establish organoid systems centred on long-term stem cell expansion and self-organised differentiation.
## Long-term expansion of adult tissue-derived airway organoids, 2017 to 2025
Early three-dimensional airway epithelial cultures could usually be maintained only briefly or passaged a limited number of times. Media optimisation later enabled long-term expansion and disease modelling with adult tissue-derived airway organoids. Tan et al. (2017) used tissue engineering methods to construct human airway organoids for studies of lung regeneration and disease. Marthin et al. (2017) generated three-dimensional explant spheroids from patient nasal epithelium for donor-specific modelling. Sachs et al. (2019) reported long-term expandable human airway organoids in the EMBO Journal. Regulation of TGF-β, FGF, and WNT signalling allowed the cultures to expand for more than a year while retaining the capacity to differentiate into basal, ciliated, and secretory lineages. The system also supported cryopreservation, recovery, and patient-specific modelling of inherited airway disorders such as cystic fibrosis.
Later studies broadened the tissue range, developmental stage, and species represented by related organoid systems. Tindle et al. (2021) developed adult stem cell-derived whole-lung organoids containing both airway and alveolar lineages. Gerli et al. (2024) used single-cell transcriptomic guidance to establish prenatal fetal epithelial organoids from amniotic fluid and tracheal fluid. Valenzuela et al. (2024) replaced Matrigel with an alginate hydrogel and generated expandable lung organoids containing proximal and distal lineages. Elbadawy et al. (2025) established bat lung organoids for cross-species studies. Not all of these systems meet a strict definition of an airway organoid, but they test the feasibility of whole-lung coverage, prenatal sampling, regional differentiation, and cross-species modelling.
## Control of polarity and access to the apical surface, 2002 to 2024
Polarity and access to the apical surface directly affect functional testing and virological use of airway organoids. Organoids embedded in extracellular matrix usually adopt an apical-in configuration, with the apical surface facing the lumen and the basal surface facing outwards. Pathogens and drugs therefore have limited direct access to the apical surface, even though this surface supports mucociliary clearance and carries many viral receptors.
Apical-out methods reverse polarity so that pathogens and drugs can contact the epithelium directly while the organoid retains its three-dimensional structure. Stroulios et al. (2022) established apical-out airway organoids for viral infection studies and antiviral drug screening. Li et al. (2024) later established apical-out nasal organoids. Dye et al. (2020) found that the physicochemical properties of polymer scaffolds could guide polarity in lung organoids. The apical-out configuration makes intact organoids more accessible for infection experiments and drug screening.

# Framework for evaluating airway organoid models
## Technical goals identified across the field
The reviews cited here converge on three technical goals:
1. Reconstruct the cellular composition and lineages of the target tissue. A model should contain the main cell types rather than merely express their markers (Wang et al., 2026; Barkauskas et al., 2017; Hughes et al., 2022).
2. Reconstruct spatial organisation and polarity. The model should reproduce tissue-relevant spatial arrangement and interface orientation, including luminal architecture, apical-basal polarity, and regional identity (Wang et al., 2026; Kim et al., 2020).
3. Test physiological function with dynamic assays. Marker expression alone is insufficient. Depending on the research question, functional tests may include ciliary movement, barrier activity, secretion, or responses to infection (Hughes et al., 2022; Vazquez-Armendariz and Tata, 2023).
The same reviews discuss maturity and agreement with tissue in vivo (Hughes et al., 2022; Vazquez-Armendariz and Tata, 2023), reproducibility and standardisation (Kim et al., 2020; Hughes et al., 2022), sample representativeness (Barkauskas et al., 2017; Hughes et al., 2022), microenvironmental fit (Wang et al., 2026; Kim et al., 2020), and stable expansion at scale (Wang et al., 2026; Vazquez-Armendariz and Tata, 2023).
## From technical goals to evaluation dimensions
The evaluation uses these dimensions:
1. Cell lineage completeness: does the model reconstruct the main airway lineages, including basal, ciliated, goblet, and club cells?
2. Spatial organisation and polarity: does the model reproduce an airway-relevant air-liquid interface and apical-basal polarity, including luminal architecture, ciliary orientation, and continuous tight junctions?
3. Physiological function: do dynamic assays test mucociliary clearance, barrier function, ion transport, infection responses, secretion, or other relevant activities?
4. Cell source: does primary tissue, an iPSC source, or another source represent the required developmental stage, airway region, donor background, and degree of personalisation? This dimension is classified but not scored.
Maturity and agreement with tissue in vivo, reproducibility and standardisation, sample representativeness, microenvironmental fit, and stable expansion at scale form the remaining quality-control dimensions. Together they assess biological correspondence and practical usability. This project applied the dimensions to 38 studies of airway organoid models, where each dimension distinguished differences between models (see the appended literature table).
# From model evaluation to respiratory virology
The meaning of a virological result depends on the quality of the model used to obtain it. Cell lineage completeness determines whether comparisons of cell tropism are valid. Spatial organisation, polarity, and access to the apical surface affect inoculation routes, entry mechanisms, and the observation of barrier injury. Dynamic functional assays determine whether ciliary motion, cytokine responses, and tissue damage can be interpreted as physiologically relevant phenotypes. Cell source, maturity, and sample representativeness also shape conclusions about donor variation, underlying disease, and viral adaptation. Each model characteristic therefore needs to be matched to the endpoint under study.
This mapping sets limits on claims that an organoid is better or irreplaceable. A simplified model may be sufficient when the question is whether a virus can replicate in a permissive cell. Organoids are more likely to add information when the question depends on airway receptors and proteases, tropism across multiple cell types, epithelial polarity, donor background, or persistent tissue remodelling. Agreement between an organoid and a primary air-liquid interface culture supports the physiological relevance of the organoid, but does not establish an exclusive advantage.
Studies of SARS-CoV-2 and influenza virus can be read through this framework by pairing each limitation of a traditional model with the feature supplied by an airway organoid and the quantitative or mechanistic evidence gained from that comparison.
# Evidence from respiratory virology
Airway organoids retain epithelial cell diversity, differentiation state, tissue polarity, barrier architecture, and the airway receptor and protease environment in an expandable and experimentally tractable human system. These properties, rather than three-dimensional shape alone, allow researchers to examine viral replication, cell tropism, tissue injury, innate immune responses, and drug effects in the same airway context. This reduces the evidential gap between cell lines with limited physiological relevance, animal models affected by species differences, and human airway explants that are difficult to obtain. Organoids can improve the relevance of virological conclusions to the human airway. In some settings, they can also identify mechanisms that a traditional model misses or misclassifies. The second claim applies only to specific questions and is not a general property of organoids.
The evidence set contains 10 studies of SARS-CoV-2 and 10 studies of influenza virus, with five core and five supporting candidates in each group. These 20 papers were selected from 113 deduplicated records published between 2018 and 2026. They cover viral entry, replicative fitness, cell tropism, host responses, donor variation, antiviral screening, neutralisation, and cross-species risk assessment ([[Part2-查准版检索报告|Part 2 precision-search report]]). The available material consists of bibliographic records and public abstracts. Experimental direction, author conclusions, and quantitative results reported in those abstracts can support a draft narrative, but experimental conditions, statistical effect sizes, and claims that a finding was possible only in organoids still require full-text verification.
Early work asked whether airway organoids could reproduce major features of the human airway. Differentiated human airway organoids established in 2018 contained ciliated, goblet, club, and basal cells and expressed higher levels of airway serine proteases involved in influenza activation. The system distinguished H7 and H1 viruses with different infectivity in humans and could therefore support risk assessment of emerging strains ([[11-Zhou-2018-Emerging-Influenza-Infectivity|Zhou et al., 2018]]). In the same year, influenza replication kinetics and cell tropism were broadly consistent between airway organoids and human bronchial explants, showing that expandable organoids could reproduce some results obtained in native tissue ([[12-Hui-2018-Influenza-in-Airway-Organoids-and-Bronchus|Hui et al., 2018]]). Later studies compared tropism across airway cell types. Influenza B virus, for example, infected ciliated, club, goblet, and basal cells, while pathogenic differences were greater between strains than between lineages ([[13-Bui-2019-Influenza-B-Tropism|Bui et al., 2019]]).
Work published from 2021 to 2023 shifted towards mechanism and donor-specific modelling. In SARS-CoV-2 research, airway organoids were used to analyse the relationship between the multibasic cleavage site and serine protease-mediated entry ([[01-Mykytyn-2021-Serine-protease-entry|Mykytyn et al., 2021]]). Researchers also combined high-content screening with transcriptomics, metabolomics, and genetic perturbation to identify the HIF1α-glycolysis axis and host mechanisms related to fatty acid synthesis ([[06-Duan-2021-HIF1a-glycolysis-screen|Duan et al., 2021]]). Donor-derived models retained some disease phenotypes. COPD bronchial organoids showed goblet cell hyperplasia, reduced ciliary beat frequency, and abnormal differentiation trajectories, together with greater productive viral replication and stronger pro-inflammatory responses after infection ([[03-Chan-2022-COPD-organoids|Chan et al., 2022]]). An airway organoid biobank from 20 donors supported comparisons across donors and variants and identified TSPAN8 as a promoter of SARS-CoV-2 infection ([[07-Hysenaj-2023-Tetraspanin-8|Hysenaj et al., 2023]]). In influenza studies, endogenous SPINK6 inhibited haemagglutinin cleavage mediated by HAT and KLK5, thereby restricting viral maturation and replication ([[14-Wang-2022-SPINK6-Restricts-Influenza-Activation|Wang et al., 2022]]).
Studies published after 2023 began testing whether conclusions from traditional models held in a human airway context and whether organoids improved translational prediction. Monkey kidney cell lines had suggested that Omicron mainly used cathepsin-dependent entry. Airway organoids showed that BA.1 and XBB1.5 still depended on type II transmembrane serine proteases in the human airway ([[04-Mykytyn-2023-Omicron-entry-proteases|Mykytyn et al., 2023]]). Nasal and airway organoids were also used to compare replication, entry, and syncytium formation among variants including BA.5 ([[08-Li-2023-Replicative-fitness|Li et al., 2023]]). A microphysiological system combining bronchial organoids with a vascular bed separated direct viral infection from vascular injury caused by epithelial paracrine signals ([[09-Fujimoto-2024-Vascular-bed-disruption|Fujimoto et al., 2024]]). For screening, apical-out airway organoids combined direct infection of intact organoids with one-organoid-per-well assays. The abstract reported an approximately tenfold increase in throughput and an approximately 100-fold reduction in cell use ([[10-Lee-2024-Apical-out-airway-organoids|Lee et al., 2024]]). Nasal organoid neutralisation assays also used clinical efficacy rather than cell-line readouts alone as a reference for prediction ([[05-Wan-2025-Organoid-neutralization|Wan et al., 2025]]).
Influenza research also moved from seasonal-virus phenotyping to the zoonotic risk posed by emerging H5 viruses. Airway organoids allowed parallel comparison of H5N6 and H5N8 replication, tropism across four main airway epithelial cell types, and inflammatory responses ([[16-Bui-2021-HPAI-Risk-Assessment|Bui et al., 2021]]). They were also used to observe fibrotic-like remodelling after persistent H5N1 infection and the effect of ROCK pathway inhibition ([[18-Rothan-2025-H5N1-Fibrotic-Remodeling|Rothan et al., 2025]]). One study used models from six donors to compare adaptation of the H5N1 D1.1 and B3.13 genotypes to human nasal and airway epithelium ([[19-Zhang-2026-H5N1-Genotype-Adaptation|Zhang et al., 2026]]). A multi-species airway organoid panel converted wildlife challenge experiments, which are difficult to perform broadly, into parallel screens of respiratory tissue susceptibility ([[20-Tarres-Freixas-2026-Influenza-Tropism-Wildlife|Tarrés-Freixas et al., 2026]]). Neutralisation assays in human airway and nasal organoids also detected cross-neutralisation by H5N1 N1 antibodies that conventional assays had not shown ([[15-Li-2026-H5N1-Cross-Species-Transmission-and-N1-Antibodies|Li et al., 2026]]).
These results show that viral genotype, airway cell composition, differentiation state, receptor and protease expression, tissue polarity, and donor background jointly shape viral behaviour. Organoids usually add more information when a question depends strongly on these host factors, although the advantage remains endpoint-specific. In a direct comparison, primary human bronchial epithelial ALI cultures and two airway organoid systems produced broadly similar H1N1 replication kinetics, antiviral cytokine responses, and serum inhibition. Viral titres rose by more than 4 log in all three models and fell by 3 to 6 log after serum pre-incubation ([[17-Ekanger-2025-ALI-versus-Airway-Organoids|Ekanger et al., 2025]]). Airway organoids therefore do not produce unique conclusions for every virological endpoint. Their value should be judged through a specific sequence: the limitation of the traditional model, the way the organoid addresses that limitation, and the evidence added by the comparison. Descriptions such as "three-dimensional" or "closer to the human body" are not sufficient.
# Advantages over traditional models
## Retaining the airway receptor and protease environment
Receptor and host protease expression in immortalised two-dimensional cell lines depends on cell origin and culture conditions. A virus may therefore favour a pathway that differs from the one used in the human airway. SARS-CoV-2 shows inconsistent protease dependence across cell lines, so cell-line experiments alone cannot identify the entry mechanism most relevant to the airway. Differentiated airway organoids retain airway epithelial cells and their membrane proteases, allowing entry to be tested in a tissue-relevant environment ([[01-Mykytyn-2021-Serine-protease-entry|Mykytyn et al., 2021]]). Omicron can enter monkey kidney cell lines through cathepsins, yet BA.1 and XBB1.5 still use type II transmembrane serine proteases in human airway organoids ([[04-Mykytyn-2023-Omicron-entry-proteases|Mykytyn et al., 2023]]). Entry preferences observed in cell lines cannot therefore be transferred directly to the human airway.
The entry environment also affects antibody neutralisation. ACE2 and TMPRSS2 expression in standard cell lines differs from that in native nasal epithelium, which may lead to underestimation of antibodies whose activity depends on a particular entry context. Nasal organoids have relatively low ACE2 and high TMPRSS2 expression. They allow class III antibodies such as VIR-7831 to be reassessed in respiratory epithelium and compared with real-world efficacy ([[05-Wan-2025-Organoid-neutralization|Wan et al., 2025]]).
## Observing viral tropism and host responses together
A single cell line can show whether one cell type permits infection, but it cannot compare viral preference across ciliated, goblet, club, and basal cells in the same system. It also provides little information about how cell tropism relates to tissue dysfunction. Differentiated airway organoids contain several major airway epithelial cell types, so replication, tropism, and cell type-specific responses can be measured in one host background. In influenza studies, the system distinguished viruses with different infectivity in humans and linked this difference to the proportion of ciliated cells and the level of airway serine proteases ([[11-Zhou-2018-Emerging-Influenza-Infectivity|Zhou et al., 2018]]). Influenza B virus infected ciliated, club, goblet, and basal cells ([[13-Bui-2019-Influenza-B-Tropism|Bui et al., 2019]]). Some H5N6 and H5N8 strains also infected all four cell types and showed strain-specific differences in tropism ([[16-Bui-2021-HPAI-Risk-Assessment|Bui et al., 2021]]). A single cell line cannot support this type of cross-cell comparison.
## Linking epithelial polarity, barrier injury, and viral phenotype
Flat cultures do not consistently reproduce apical-basal polarity, ciliary motion, tight junctions, or the spatial relationships between airway epithelial cells. They can measure viral load but reveal less about how a virus damages the airway barrier. Differentiated nasal organoids formed cellular compositions and structures that resembled native nasal epithelium. In this model, Omicron had greater infectivity and replicative fitness than earlier variants, while both Delta and Omicron damaged ciliated cells and disrupted tight junctions. The model linked variant replication with epithelial barrier injury ([[02-Chiu-2022-Human-nasal-organoids|Chiu et al., 2022]]). Nasal and airway organoids also allowed entry efficiency, syncytium formation, and replicative fitness of BA.5 to be compared in the same epithelial system. The ancestral strain and B.1.1.529 showed less prominent syncytial phenotypes ([[08-Li-2023-Replicative-fitness|Li et al., 2023]]). The additional information extends beyond viral titre to the tissue consequences of molecular entry events.
Three-dimensional structure also creates practical limitations. In conventional organoids, the apical surface faces inwards, which complicates viral inoculation and drug screening. Polarity reversal in apical-out airway organoids exposes an intact differentiated epithelium directly to the virus. Dose responses to three antiviral drugs were comparable to those obtained in ALI culture, while the abstract reported an approximately tenfold increase in throughput and an approximately 100-fold reduction in cell use ([[10-Lee-2024-Apical-out-airway-organoids|Lee et al., 2024]]). The improvement comes from engineering the three-dimensional structure rather than from three-dimensional shape alone.
## Retaining patient and donor variation
Standard cell lines usually represent a single clonal background, while animal models add species-specific differences in receptors, proteases, and immunity. Neither approach directly captures how underlying disease and population heterogeneity alter airway infection. Donor-derived airway organoids can retain some individual phenotypes in vitro. COPD-derived organoids showed goblet cell hyperplasia, reduced ciliary beat frequency, and abnormal differentiation trajectories. After SARS-CoV-2 infection, COPD bronchial organoids supported greater productive viral replication and stronger pro-inflammatory responses to viral or bacterial exposure ([[03-Chan-2022-COPD-organoids|Chan et al., 2022]]). The comparison provides a defined chain of evidence: a standard model lacks disease background, patient-derived organoids retain disease-associated epithelial phenotypes, and infection experiments test whether those phenotypes accompany altered susceptibility. A phenotypically stable airway organoid biobank from 20 donors ([[07-Hysenaj-2023-Tetraspanin-8|Hysenaj et al., 2023]]) and a comparison of H5N1 D1.1 and B3.13 in nasal and airway organoids from six donors ([[19-Zhang-2026-H5N1-Genotype-Adaptation|Zhang et al., 2026]]) incorporated donor replication into assessments of viral adaptation instead of extrapolating from one cell line to an entire population.
## Combining tissue origin with scalable culture
Human bronchial explants have high physiological relevance but depend on clinical sampling. They cannot be expanded over long periods and are poorly suited to repeated large comparisons. Airway organoids can be established from adult or pluripotent stem cells, expanded, and differentiated as three-dimensional structures or two-dimensional monolayers when needed. Hui et al. found that influenza replication kinetics and cell tropism in organoids broadly resembled those in human bronchial explants, supporting their use as a reproducible human alternative when native tissue is scarce ([[12-Hui-2018-Influenza-in-Airway-Organoids-and-Bronchus|Hui et al., 2018]]). Nasal organoids developed by Chiu et al. could be passaged for more than six months and established from relatively accessible nasal epithelial cells ([[02-Chiu-2022-Human-nasal-organoids|Chiu et al., 2022]]). These models also support high-content compound screening, followed by transcriptomic, metabolic, and genetic analysis of selected compounds ([[06-Duan-2021-HIF1a-glycolysis-screen|Duan et al., 2021]]). Airway organoids reduce the conflict between limited access to human bronchial tissue and the need for repeatable experiments.
## Analysing tissue interactions and persistent pathological processes
A single cell line or an isolated vascular model cannot readily distinguish remote tissue injury caused by direct viral infection from injury caused by paracrine signals released by infected epithelium. When bronchial organoids were co-cultured with a vascular bed, infection of the organoids damaged vascular structures. Direct addition of the virus to the vascular bed caused neither infection nor injury. Knockout of interferon-related genes or inhibition of JAK/STAT signalling reduced the effect, implicating epithelial cytokines in vascular damage ([[09-Fujimoto-2024-Vascular-bed-disruption|Fujimoto et al., 2024]]). This co-culture design separates epithelial infection from downstream vascular responses under controlled conditions.
Short two-dimensional infection experiments are also poorly suited to continuous observation of tissue remodelling after viral replication. Persistent H5N1 infection in human airway organoids increased α-SMA, TGF-β, and several extracellular matrix-related markers, while ROCK inhibition reduced fibrogenesis ([[18-Rothan-2025-H5N1-Fibrotic-Remodeling|Rothan et al., 2025]]). The same system can therefore follow viral replication, inflammation, fibrotic-like remodelling, and the effect of pathway inhibition over time.
