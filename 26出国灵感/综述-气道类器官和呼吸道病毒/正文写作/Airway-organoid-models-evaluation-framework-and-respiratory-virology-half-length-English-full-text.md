# Abstract
Airway organoids retain selected structural and functional properties of airway epithelium in expandable human systems. They complement two-dimensional cell culture, animal models, and human airway explants. This review first defines the origin, capabilities, and limits of organoids, then evaluates them through cell lineage composition, spatial organisation, polarity, physiological function, maturity, reproducibility, sample representativeness, microenvironmental fit, and expansion capacity. It then examines studies of SARS-CoV-2 and influenza virus. Organoids often add information when a question depends on airway cell composition, receptor and protease expression, tissue polarity, or donor background. For some endpoints involving replication, cytokine responses, and neutralisation, however, organoids and air-liquid interface cultures produce similar results. The value of a model therefore depends on the research question and the evidence it adds. The virological evidence discussed here comes mainly from bibliographic records and public abstracts, so claims of exclusivity still require full-text verification.
# Definition, source, and limits of organoids
Organoids are structures formed through the self-organisation of stem or progenitor cells in three-dimensional culture. They selectively reproduce some structural and functional properties of the tissue of origin (Wang et al., 2026; Kim et al., 2020). They are not complete organs in miniature. Vazquez-Armendariz and Tata (2023) likewise included expansion, differentiation, and partial tissue recapitulation in their definition of lung organoids.
Organoids occupy a position between two-dimensional cell culture and animal models. Two-dimensional systems are easy to manipulate but lack tissue architecture. Animal models preserve more of the tissue environment but introduce species differences. Human organoids can be expanded and genetically manipulated in vitro, making them useful for human-specific studies of development, metabolism, and drug response. They complement rather than replace native tissue and animal experiments (Kim et al., 2020).
The two main sources are pluripotent stem cells, including PSCs and iPSCs, and tissue stem cells, including TSCs and AdSCs. The PSC route models developmental signalling and can generate several lineages, although the cells often remain fetal-like. The TSC route starts from the target tissue and more closely resembles the adult phenotype, but tissue access is limited and the resulting models are mainly epithelial (Wang et al., 2026; Kim et al., 2020). Source is a classification variable because it determines developmental stage, airway region, and donor background. It is not a direct measure of model quality.
Media supplements and co-culture conditions also affect adult, fetal, and iPSC-derived models. Identical starting cells can reach different endpoints under different culture conditions. iPSCs support scale-up and experimental control of development, whereas adult-derived models are more likely to retain features associated with donor age and disease (Vazquez-Armendariz and Tata, 2023). Source selection should begin with the airway region, maturity, and population that the study needs to represent.
Organoids remain reductionist models. They usually lack vasculature, immune cells, and stroma, and cannot reproduce all systemic variables (Wang et al., 2026; Hughes et al., 2022). Complexity should follow the research question because unnecessary co-culture adds variation. Orthogonal models or independent datasets are still needed before extending organoid findings beyond their experimental context (Kim et al., 2020).
# Technical development and evaluation of airway organoids
Organoid technology developed along two main routes. In 2009, Sato et al. showed that a single Lgr5+ adult intestinal stem cell could form a crypt-villus-like structure without a mesenchymal niche, establishing the tissue stem cell route (Sato et al., 2009). In 2013, Lancaster et al. generated cerebral organoids containing several brain regions from human pluripotent stem cells and used them to model microcephaly, establishing a disease-modelling use for the PSC route (Lancaster et al., 2013). The first route more closely resembles adult tissue, while the second is better suited to developmental studies. Their maturity cannot be judged on one scale.
Airway research first addressed three-dimensional morphology and polarity. Collagen-gel and bronchial epithelial spheroid cultures from 2002 to 2007 showed polarised CFTR expression and differentiated airway surface epithelium. Primary basal cells, dual-SMAD expansion, and directed hPSC differentiation later addressed cell source and expansion. In 2019, Sachs et al. regulated TGF-β, FGF, and WNT signalling to establish human airway organoids that expanded for more than a year while retaining basal, ciliated, and secretory differentiation. The system also supported cryopreservation and patient-specific disease modelling.
Related studies broadened tissue range and developmental stage. Adult stem cell-derived models contained airway and alveolar lineages, while amniotic and tracheal fluid provided prenatal fetal epithelial organoids. Alginate hydrogel was also tested as an alternative to Matrigel, and bat lung organoids extended related methods to cross-species studies. These systems are not all airway organoids under a strict definition, but they test modelling across sources, regions, and species.
Conventional extracellular matrix-embedded organoids usually have an apical-in configuration, so viruses and drugs have limited access to the luminal surface. Polarity reversal exposes that surface in apical-out organoids and improves access for infection and drug screening (Stroulios et al., 2022; Li et al., 2024). Three-dimensional structure does not create an automatic advantage. Experimental access depends on how polarity is engineered.
Evaluation starts with four questions. Does the model contain the main basal, ciliated, goblet, and club cell lineages? Does it reproduce airway-relevant spatial organisation, apical-basal polarity, and tight junctions? Do dynamic assays test ciliary motion, barrier function, ion transport, secretion, or infection responses? Does the source represent the required developmental stage, airway region, and donor background? Source is classified but not scored. Maturity, agreement with tissue in vivo, reproducibility, sample representativeness, microenvironmental fit, and stable expansion form the remaining quality-control dimensions (Wang et al., 2026; Kim et al., 2020; Hughes et al., 2022; Vazquez-Armendariz and Tata, 2023).
Marker expression cannot replace functional testing. Culture duration, diameter, and colony-forming efficiency mainly describe culture performance. This project applied the dimensions to 38 airway organoid studies and found that each could distinguish model characteristics, although interpretation still depends on the research aim. The framework does not seek one total score. Entry studies need appropriate receptors, proteases, and polarity, while screening also depends on reproducibility, handling, and throughput. Performance in one task does not imply superiority at every endpoint.
# From model quality to virological interpretation
Each model dimension maps to a virological endpoint. Cell lineages affect comparisons of tropism. Polarity and apical access affect inoculation and entry. Dynamic function determines whether barrier injury and cytokine responses have physiological meaning, while source and maturity affect conclusions about donors, underlying disease, and viral adaptation. A simplified model should remain in use when it already answers the question. An organoid has an advantage only when its features change the conclusion or add testable information.
Model comparison should connect three points: what the traditional model lacks, what the organoid supplies, and what evidence the comparison adds. Closer agreement with native tissue at one endpoint does not establish general superiority. Agreement with ALI culture supports physiological relevance but cannot by itself show that an organoid is irreplaceable. Organoids also contain internal compromises. More cell types may improve tissue relevance while increasing culture variation, and apical-out access still requires evidence that polarity reversal has not altered the phenotype under study.
# Evidence added to respiratory virus research
The evidence set contains 10 studies of SARS-CoV-2 and 10 studies of influenza virus selected from 113 deduplicated records ([[Part2-查准版检索报告|Part 2 precision-search report]]). The analysis retains directions and quantitative results reported in abstracts without converting an abstract-level advantage into an exclusive claim.
The questions changed as the models matured. Studies around 2018 compared organoids with human bronchial explants. Work after 2021 combined omics, genetic perturbation, and donor banks to study entry and host factors. Studies after 2023 increasingly tested conclusions from cell lines, increased screening throughput, and assessed emerging H5 viruses. The change made the relation between model and question more specific rather than simply making the model more complex.
## Entry mechanisms and neutralisation
Receptor and host protease expression in immortalised cell lines varies with cell origin and culture conditions, which can produce entry preferences that do not apply to the human airway. Differentiated airway organoids retain epithelial membrane proteases and allow the relationship between the SARS-CoV-2 multibasic cleavage site and serine protease-mediated entry to be tested ([[01-Mykytyn-2021-Serine-protease-entry|Mykytyn et al., 2021]]). Monkey kidney cell lines had suggested that Omicron mainly used cathepsins, yet BA.1 and XBB1.5 still used type II transmembrane serine proteases in human airway organoids ([[04-Mykytyn-2023-Omicron-entry-proteases|Mykytyn et al., 2023]]). Nasal organoids, with relatively low ACE2 and high TMPRSS2 expression, also allowed class III antibodies such as VIR-7831 to be reassessed against clinical efficacy ([[05-Wan-2025-Organoid-neutralization|Wan et al., 2025]]).
The entry environment affects both drug and antibody readouts. Receptor overexpression or a different protease pathway can shift neutralisation results away from native nasal epithelium. Organoids provide a more appropriate validation context in this setting, but they do not replace every standardised neutralisation assay. Claims of exclusivity still need full text and direct model comparisons.
## Cell tropism, polarity, and barrier injury
The cellular composition of airway organoids allows replication, tropism, and tissue injury to be compared in one background. Differentiated organoids distinguished H7 and H1 viruses with different infectivity in humans ([[11-Zhou-2018-Emerging-Influenza-Infectivity|Zhou et al., 2018]]), and their influenza replication kinetics and tropism broadly matched human bronchial explants ([[12-Hui-2018-Influenza-in-Airway-Organoids-and-Bronchus|Hui et al., 2018]]). Influenza B virus infected ciliated, club, goblet, and basal cells ([[13-Bui-2019-Influenza-B-Tropism|Bui et al., 2019]]). Some H5N6 and H5N8 strains also showed strain-specific tropism across these four cell types ([[16-Bui-2021-HPAI-Risk-Assessment|Bui et al., 2021]]).
In nasal organoids, Delta and Omicron damaged ciliated cells and disrupted tight junctions ([[02-Chiu-2022-Human-nasal-organoids|Chiu et al., 2022]]). Nasal and airway organoids also placed BA.5 entry, syncytium formation, and replicative fitness in the same epithelial context ([[08-Li-2023-Replicative-fitness|Li et al., 2023]]). A single cell line cannot support this cross-cell comparison or connect tropism with tissue dysfunction. The additional information comes from cell diversity and spatial structure rather than from a replication readout alone.
## Host mechanisms and donor variation
High-content screening combined with transcriptomics, metabolomics, and genetic perturbation identified the HIF1α-glycolysis axis and host mechanisms related to fatty acid synthesis ([[06-Duan-2021-HIF1a-glycolysis-screen|Duan et al., 2021]]). In influenza studies, endogenous SPINK6 inhibited haemagglutinin cleavage mediated by HAT and KLK5, restricting viral maturation and replication ([[14-Wang-2022-SPINK6-Restricts-Influenza-Activation|Wang et al., 2022]]).
Donor-derived models retained some individual differences. COPD bronchial organoids showed goblet cell hyperplasia, reduced ciliary beat frequency, and abnormal differentiation, followed by greater viral replication and stronger pro-inflammatory responses after infection ([[03-Chan-2022-COPD-organoids|Chan et al., 2022]]). A biobank from 20 donors supported comparisons across donors and variants and identified TSPAN8 as a promoter of infection ([[07-Hysenaj-2023-Tetraspanin-8|Hysenaj et al., 2023]]). These models include disease background and donor replication, but do not represent all population variation.
## Native tissue features and scale
Human bronchial explants are physiologically relevant but depend on clinical sampling and cannot support long-term expansion or repeated large comparisons. Influenza replication kinetics and tropism in airway organoids broadly matched bronchial explants ([[12-Hui-2018-Influenza-in-Airway-Organoids-and-Bronchus|Hui et al., 2018]]). Nasal organoids could be passaged for more than six months and established from accessible nasal epithelium ([[02-Chiu-2022-Human-nasal-organoids|Chiu et al., 2022]]). Expandable human models also support high-content screening followed by transcriptomic, metabolic, and genetic analysis of selected compounds ([[06-Duan-2021-HIF1a-glycolysis-screen|Duan et al., 2021]]). This addresses tissue scarcity and experimental repetition without reproducing the whole native tissue.
## Screening, tissue interactions, and persistent pathology
Apical-out airway organoids expose intact epithelium directly to virus. Their dose responses to three antiviral drugs matched ALI culture, while the abstract reported an approximately tenfold increase in throughput and an approximately 100-fold reduction in cell use ([[10-Lee-2024-Apical-out-airway-organoids|Lee et al., 2024]]). In a co-culture of bronchial organoids and a vascular bed, infected epithelium damaged vascular structures through paracrine signalling. Direct exposure of the vascular bed caused neither infection nor injury, and JAK/STAT inhibition reduced the effect ([[09-Fujimoto-2024-Vascular-bed-disruption|Fujimoto et al., 2024]]). Persistent H5N1 infection also increased α-SMA, TGF-β, and extracellular matrix markers, while ROCK inhibition reduced fibrogenesis ([[18-Rothan-2025-H5N1-Fibrotic-Remodeling|Rothan et al., 2025]]). Organoids can therefore follow epithelial infection, remote tissue responses, and remodelling in a controlled system.
## Emerging H5 viruses and cross-species risk
Nasal and airway organoids from six donors were used to compare adaptation of H5N1 D1.1 and B3.13 to human epithelium ([[19-Zhang-2026-H5N1-Genotype-Adaptation|Zhang et al., 2026]]). Multi-species airway organoid panels converted wildlife challenge experiments, which are difficult to perform broadly, into parallel screens of respiratory tissue susceptibility ([[20-Tarres-Freixas-2026-Influenza-Tropism-Wildlife|Tarrés-Freixas et al., 2026]]). Human airway and nasal organoids also revealed cross-neutralisation by H5N1 N1 antibodies that conventional assays had not shown ([[15-Li-2026-H5N1-Cross-Species-Transmission-and-N1-Antibodies|Li et al., 2026]]). These findings identify risk signals, but cross-species inference still needs other models.
## Endpoint limits
Airway organoids do not produce unique conclusions at every endpoint. In a direct comparison, primary human bronchial epithelial ALI culture and two airway organoid systems produced similar H1N1 replication kinetics, antiviral cytokine responses, and serum inhibition. Viral titres rose by more than 4 log in all three models and fell by 3 to 6 log after serum pre-incubation ([[17-Ekanger-2025-ALI-versus-Airway-Organoids|Ekanger et al., 2025]]). This counterexample defines the boundary of organoid advantage. Evaluation should state what the traditional model lacks, what the organoid supplies, and what evidence the comparison adds. Labels such as "three-dimensional" or "closer to the human body" are not sufficient.
