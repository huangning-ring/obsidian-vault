# 核心逻辑链与策略分析
**综述的真实论点。** 这篇文献要回答的不是"类器官很厉害"，而是一个产业问题：类器官凭什么可以进入受监管的药物开发流程。 **作者给出的答案由三根支柱构成**，全篇所有案例都是这三根支柱的证据，而非并列的技术清单：
- 不可替代的预测生态位——人特有机制
- 可重复与可规模化——工程化与自动化
- 监管可接受性——新方法学（NAM）的资格认定 **核心论证模式。** 每个疾病案例都遵循同一句潜台词：小鼠在这里不成表型，人细胞系又丢掉了组织结构，所以这个问题只能在人类类器官里问。 **这个模式被反复夯实**，演讲必须把它讲出来，听众才会记住结构而不是记住例子：
- CFTR 突变小鼠不出现严重气道病变
- _Dnajc6_ 敲除小鼠无帕金森表型
- 小鼠天然抵抗人诺如病毒
- 小鼠不携带乳糜泻相关 HLA-DQ 单倍型
- ZIKV NS5 只靶向人 STAT2，不靶向小鼠 STAT2 **三个深挖点各锁死一种怀疑**，缺任何一环论证都会被听众一句话打回：
- 囊性纤维化——能不能预测**这个患者**的临床反应
- MCLA-158——能不能真的**产出一个药**
- 平台工程——能不能**规模化并测出毒性** **实验策略层面最值得剖析的三个巧思：**
- 把分子功能翻译成几何读出：福斯高林升 cAMP，CFTR 介导水内流，通道功能变成可成像的体积变化
- 把治疗窗内建进筛选第一步：MCLA-158 筛选时同步用配对健康结肠类器官作对照，选择性即命中标准本身
- 把结构拆开以换取可及性：翻转、摊平、通道化三种解法，牺牲部分三维性换取 TEER、双向转运与 ADME 能力 **向下一步的推演**（标注为推演，非原文结论）。综述自承的缺口指向三条必然路径：
- 多项在研随机试验将首次给出类器官指导用药的前瞻性读出
- 阴性预测数据的系统性报告是划定预测边界的前提
- 系统性药代只能靠"流体连接的多器官平台 + 定量系统药理学建模"补足，单一类器官原理上无法回答

# 中文演讲稿：人类类器官作为药物发现的三维体外平台
## 【0:00-04:00】开场：一个持续失败的环节
各位同事，早上好。我想从一个让所有做药的人不舒服的事实开始。 **在临床前看起来很好的化合物，进入人体后仍然大量失败。** 而失败最集中的地方——肝毒性、心毒性、中枢神经系统不良反应——恰恰是我们投入几十年、建立了最多标准化试验的领域。这说明问题不在勤奋，而在模型。 **我们手上长期只有两类主力模型，各有一条硬边界。** **第一类是二维细胞系。** 它便宜、稳定、随处可得。但请注意它的出身：细胞系几乎无例外地经历过某种恶性转化，要么本身来自肿瘤，要么为了贴在塑料上长期生长而被反复驯化。 **它没有组织结构，也不含目标器官应有的那一整套功能细胞类型。** 有一个观察特别说明问题：把细胞系移植到体内，它形成肿瘤；把类器官移植进去，它形成健康的、有功能的组织。 **第二类是动物模型。** 它给了我们二维系统永远给不了的东西——完整生物体的语境，包括系统性药代、器官间对话和整体病理。 **但物种差异是硬边界。** 许多人类疾病在动物身上根本无法复现，而生理差异直接限制了对人体安全性和有效性的预测。 **与此同时，合规压力在快速上升：** 美国国会先后通过 FDA 现代化法案 2.0 与 3.0，用以澄清动物模型在药物开发中是否为必需；FDA 更宣布计划在单克隆抗体等药物开发中逐步淘汰动物试验。替代方案不再只是学术偏好，它正在变成合规需求。 **于是问题非常具体：** 有没有一种模型，既保留人类组织的结构与细胞多样性，又能像细胞系一样被扩增、被编辑、被高通量筛选？ **我要先说清立场，免得听起来像宣传。** 类器官不是万能替代品，它是一个高度还原论的系统。我今天真正想论证三件事：它在哪些问题上不可替代、它的可重复性和规模化差到什么程度、它离监管认可还有多远。
***
## 【04:00-09:00】两条技术路线，以及第一个策略决策
**定义很简单。** 类器官是来源于干细胞、能在三维条件下自组织、并重现对应组织关键结构与功能的三维结构。但通往它的路有两条，起点完全不同，这一点常被混为一谈。 **第一条路从多能干细胞出发。** 思想源头是三十多年前对胚胎干细胞的分离，以及 2006 年山中伸弥用四个转录因子把成体体细胞重编程为诱导多能干细胞——后者一举解决了人类胚胎干细胞的伦理与来源困境。 **真正的转折发生在从二维培养转向三维聚集体的时候。** 佐佐木裕之团队发现，多能干细胞聚集起来会自发形成远比预期复杂的结构，他们造出了类视网膜和多个中枢神经系统区域的类器官。 **这条路的策略本质是：你必须按顺序重演胚胎发育。** 先用合适的信号因子把细胞导向某一胚层，再用组织特异性的发育线索导向具体细胞类型。
- 代价：时间长（数周到数月），成熟度有限（通常停留在发育晚期胎儿阶段）
- 收益：覆盖脑、视网膜、肺、肾、肝、肠，**尤其是那些没有活跃组织干细胞的组织** **第二条路从组织干细胞出发。** 几乎所有器官都靠自己的组织干细胞替换损耗细胞，主要例外是心肌、肾小球和中枢神经系统的大部分。造血干细胞最早被发现，随后人们在皮肤、角膜、肠道上皮中相继找到上皮组织干细胞。 **2009 年我们证明，来自肠上皮的单个组织干细胞就能建立不断扩增、包含所有相关肠道细胞类型的上皮类器官。** 这条路的策略优势非常直接：细胞取自目标器官的活检，因此完全跳过谱系定命这一步，只需支持长期扩增的因子，建立时间从数月压缩到数天至数周。 **我要强调这个二分法，因为它是后面所有工作的选择原则：**
- 要脑、要视网膜、要心肌——只能走多能干细胞路线
- 要消化道、气道、前列腺、肿瘤——组织干细胞路线更快更稳 **无论走哪条，疾病突变都可以用 CRISPR 引入，但有一个约束。** 编辑后的细胞必须有很强的增殖能力才能从单细胞长起来，所以基因编辑通常在多能干细胞或组织干细胞阶段完成，而不是在已分化的类器官上做。 **和传统模型比，权衡关系是清楚的。** 类器官在生理相关性上高于细胞系，在通量和成本上优于动物；但在通量上不如细胞系，在系统语境上不如动物。 **而在监管接受度上——这是关键——它还只是"正在获得承认"，动物模型则早已确立。** 这句话是我整场报告的张力所在。

## 【09:00-15:00】疾病建模全景：判据比清单重要
**类器官已被用于遗传病、代谢病、慢性炎症性疾病、感染和肿瘤。** 我不打算逐一罗列，我想给各位一条判据。 **什么时候你应该放下小鼠，转向人类类器官？答案是：当致病机制本身是人特有的时候。** 请看这条在综述中反复出现的证据链：
- **囊性纤维化**——携带 CFTR 突变（含 Δ508）的小鼠不出现患者那种严重气道病变，没有黏液堆积、气道阻塞或慢性肺部感染
- **帕金森病**——携带 _DNAJC6_ 功能缺失突变的人中脑类器官出现多巴胺神经元发育障碍、α-突触核蛋白聚集、内在放电频率升高、线粒体与溶酶体功能障碍，机制指向 WNT–LMX1A 受损；同基因敲除小鼠不重现表型
- **乳糜泻**——依赖特定 HLA-DQ 单倍型，小鼠天然不携带；即使人源化转基因小鼠也不能完整复现免疫反应与病理
- **人诺如病毒**——全球急性胃肠炎首要病因，传统细胞系不支持复制，小鼠因受体与免疫应答差异天然抵抗；人肠道类器官提供第一个体外平台，并把靶细胞定位到肠上皮吸收细胞
- **寨卡病毒**——NS5 特异性靶向人 STAT2 以抑制 I 型干扰素信号，而不靶向小鼠 STAT2 **各位听出这个模式了吗？** 五个疾病、五个器官，论证结构完全一样。类器官的生态位不是"比小鼠好"，而是"在人特有机制这一类问题上，小鼠没有答案"。 **我再举一个被临床反过来验证的例子，我认为它最有说服力。** 羟氯喹作为内吞抑制剂被提出用于 COVID-19，依据来自细胞系研究，并被广泛处方。 **但在类器官模型中，这个药不抑制病毒复制。** 后续用 CRISPR 改造的类器官生物样本库进一步证明，SARS-CoV-2、MERS-CoV 和 SARS-CoV 都特异性利用 TMPRSS2 蛋白水解激活刺突蛋白，直接完成包膜与细胞膜融合——它们根本不需要内吞途径，所以抑制内吞当然无效。 **而临床结果最终确认羟氯喹对 SARS-CoV-2 无效。** 这个案例的分量在于：类器官给出的答案与临床一致，而细胞系给出的答案把整个领域带偏了；同时它把 TMPRSS2 指认为泛冠状病毒治疗的有吸引力靶点。 **肿瘤方面，逻辑同样清晰。** 许多癌种——胰腺、肝、乳腺——在动物身上不自发成瘤；人工诱导耗时，且仍然无法复现人类肿瘤的复杂性、遗传多样性和免疫环境。 **患者来源肿瘤类器官几乎可以从所有癌种的活检或手术标本建立**，可规模化，并在组织学、遗传学和转录组层面高度保留原始肿瘤特征。 **有一个非常实用的技术细节值得注意。** 携带 KRAS G12V 突变的类器官可以在没有表皮生长因子的条件下生长，而正常类器官必需 EGF——这被普遍用来把肿瘤来源的生长从正常组织生长中区分出来。这不是花招，这是用生物学逻辑替代人工纯化。

## 【15:00-21:00】深挖案例一：囊性纤维化——把通道功能变成一个可测的体积
**现在我要慢下来，把一个案例讲透**，因为它是整个领域唯一走完"建模→预测→个体化治疗落地"全程的疾病。 **囊性纤维化是 CFTR 基因功能缺失导致的常染色体隐性遗传病。** CFTR 编码氯/碳酸氢盐通道，对包括肺和肠在内的多种上皮组织的离子与液体稳态至关重要。 **已有多种 CFTR 调节剂上市**，例如由 ivacaftor 与 lumacaftor 组成的 Orkambi，专门针对 Δ508 纯合患者——约占囊性纤维化人群的一半。 **但这里出现了精准医学的核心难题：携带同一个突变的患者，对同一个药的反应差别很大**，因为调节剂活性受细胞背景和遗传背景影响。而小鼠帮不上忙，原因我刚才说过。 **那么，怎么在体外测出"这个患者的通道现在通不通"？请欣赏这个设计。** CFTR 由 cAMP 激活；福斯高林能刺激腺苷酸环化酶，把 ATP 转化为 cAMP。 **健康的肠道或气道类器官加入福斯高林后，cAMP 升高，CFTR 被激活，水经 CFTR 依赖途径涌入管腔，类器官迅速膨胀。** 而来自囊性纤维化患者的类器官，加福斯高林不膨胀。 **这个设计有三重巧思：**
- 把**分子层面**的通道功能翻译成**几何层面**的体积变化，不需要电生理、不需要标记，一台显微镜就能读
- 读出是**连续、定量**的，因此可以刻画"部分恢复"，而不是只给阴阳性
- 正因为简单，它天然适合**高通量筛选** **接下来是逻辑推进的关键：膨胀失败可以被两条路径挽救。** 一是用 CRISPR 修正 CFTR 突变——腺嘌呤碱基编辑器已在囊性纤维化类器官生物样本库中成功纠正无义突变；二是用有效的 CFTR 调节剂处理。 **而最重要的一步是外推验证。** 在这个膨胀试验中测到的药物反应，与患者的临床结局相关，包括第一秒用力呼气容积占预计值百分比（ppFEV1）和痰细胞计数。 请体会这一步的份量。 它不再是"我的模型长得像疾病"，而是"我的模型能预测这个人吃这个药会不会好转"。正是在这里，直肠类器官支持了个体化治疗，囊性纤维化成为第一个由患者来源类器官实现个体化医疗路径的疾病。 **这个策略随后被推广到相邻的气道遗传病，读出的选择逻辑完全一致——找一个能被直接看见的功能表型：**
- **原发性纤毛运动障碍**由纤毛结构基因突变引起，包括 DNAH5/11、DNAI1、CCDC39/40；患者鼻气道类器官允许直接观察纤毛摆动，并用电子显微镜观察结构改变；在该模型中通过引导编辑纠正 DNAH11 突变，验证了基因治疗的可能性
- **SFTPB 缺陷**患者的诱导多能干细胞来源肺类器官重现了特征性的板层体异常形成，并通过重新表达野生型 SFTPB 得到挽救

## 【21:00-25:00】深挖案例二：MCLA-158——类器官到底能不能产出一个药
**听到这里，一定有人在想那个最尖锐的问题：这些都是模型，有没有一个真正的药是靠类器官做出来的？** 有，而且开发全程都在类器官上完成。 **MCLA-158（petosemtamab）是一个双特异性抗体**，一臂靶向表皮生长因子受体家族成员，另一臂靶向干细胞标志物 LGR5。 **整个发现计划完全使用患者来源的结肠癌类器官系：**
- 生成超过 500 个双特异性抗体的库
- 在一小组结直肠癌类器官系上做无偏功能筛选，读出为生长抑制与形态学改变
- 由此锁定 MCLA-158，再在更广泛的健康与结直肠癌类器官集合中做进一步表征 **筛选设计里有一个决定是整个案例的精髓：健康结肠类器官从一开始就作为对照与肿瘤类器官并行。** 结果是 MCLA-158 清除结直肠癌类器官，但不清除健康结肠类器官。 **也就是说，治疗窗不是后期安全性研究才发现的，而是被写进了命中标准本身。** 这与在细胞系上先找"最强杀伤"、再回头补做选择性的传统顺序，是根本不同的策略。 **后续结果支持了这个选择。** MCLA-158 在多种上皮肿瘤的临床前模型中显示抑制 KRAS 突变型结直肠癌生长、阻断转移起始、抑制肿瘤再生长的特性；从发现到 I 期只用了 30 个月。 **目前在进行的临床研究包括：**
- 一线头颈部鳞状细胞癌 III 期注册试验（单药，以及与帕博利珠单抗联用）
- 2024 年底启动的后线转移性结直肠癌 II 期开放标签试验 **更广泛地说，患者来源肿瘤类器官及其配对健康类器官已在多种肿瘤类型中大量建库**，同时反映肿瘤间和肿瘤内异质性，可用于研究肿瘤演化、检验治疗反应变异性、理解耐药机制和发现新靶点。 **重要的是，这些肿瘤类器官对抗肿瘤治疗的敏感性与其亲本肿瘤组织相当**；在胰腺癌、胃肠道肿瘤和乳腺癌中，类器官药物反应与临床结局之间已观察到相关性。 **但我必须同时说清楚边界：**
- 在肿瘤学之外，**转化证据仍然稀缺，受控的临床验证基本缺失**
- **阴性预测数据很少被报告**，这直接限制了我们对类器官预测边界的认识
- 目前有多项随机临床试验正在进行，尤其在放疗和化疗场景下评估其预测价值——它们的读出将决定这个领域的可信度上限

## 【25:00-31:00】深挖案例三：平台工程——为什么我们要把类器官翻过来、摊平、接上管子
**第三个案例不是一个疾病，而是一类工程决策。** 我想用它回答"能不能规模化、能不能测毒性"这个问题。 **先说一个很多人忽略的物理障碍。** 常规类器官包埋在 Matrigel 里——顺便解释一下，Matrigel 是从小鼠肿瘤中提取的可溶性基底膜制备物，富含层粘连蛋白 111、IV 型胶原和纤连蛋白，能支持细胞极性与存活。 **问题在于，这样培养的类器官基底侧朝外、顶端朝内；而病原体受体、药物转运体和通道恰恰不对称地分布在顶端表面。** 结果就是：你想让病毒或化合物接触真正的靶面，必须显微注射进管腔。 **针对这个矛盾，领域给出了三种解法，每一种都是一次明确的取舍：**
- **翻转类器官**——去掉 Matrigel 让顶端朝外，既保留三维结构又解决递送与扩散
- **摊平成二维单层**——起始细胞团仍在三维培养中产生，然后铺在 transwell 上，顶端与基底侧同时可及；牺牲三维性，换来孔间变异降低、成像更快、以及跨上皮电阻（TEER）这种非侵入、动态的屏障完整性读出（也可用荧光染料在屏障上的扩散做基于图像的替代测定）
- **通道化**——微流控平台通过带进出口的多腔室，从顶端与基底侧两侧精确控制物质递送 **顺带说一句我们最近的工作：一种细菌蛋白——耶尔森菌侵袭素——可以涂在二维 transwell 上，完全取代 Matrigel。** 这对标准化的意义不小。 **再说复杂度这一维。绝大多数类器官模型缺三样关键组织成分：血管、免疫细胞和神经。** 这不是小缺陷：多数类器官没有血管，营养和氧递送受限，直接影响生长、成熟和功能，尤其在较大的类器官中；而多数药物是静脉给药的，没有血管化就难以准确评价药物暴露。 **应对策略有三条：**
- 与内皮细胞共培养（三维生物打印、微工程组装体）
- 用微流控制造血流通道（器官芯片）
- 把巨噬细胞、树突状细胞、T 细胞等免疫细胞群体整合进培养体系 **这些工程努力已经换来了常规模型给不出的安全性信号，我举两个我认为最重要的。** **第一，免疫功能完备、血管化的肾类器官芯片系统，灌注了外周血单核细胞。** 针对 HLA-A2 呈递的 WT1 肽的 T 细胞双特异性抗体在其中选择性杀伤表达 WT1 的细胞，说明这个系统可以在动态微环境中同时评价化学性毒性和免疫介导毒性。 **第二，在肠道方向，患者来源类器官与肿瘤类器官补充免疫细胞后，显示出 T 细胞双特异性抗体的组织特异性"靶点在、肿瘤不在"毒性**，成功捕捉到了缺乏免疫成分的常规组织模型预测不到的临床不良反应；进一步地，把上皮类器官与自体组织驻留记忆 T 细胞整合成人肠道免疫类器官，被用于研究肿瘤患者中观察到的双抗诱导的肠道炎症。 **请注意这条论证的方向变了。** 前面我们说类器官能补动物模型的物种差异；这里我们说的是——对于依赖人类免疫细胞与人类上皮相互作用的新型生物药，临床前根本不存在有交叉反应的物种。在这里类器官不是替代品，而是唯一可用的工具。 **最后是规模化。传统做法是把类器官包埋在基质里、点进多孔板**，机器人和液体处理系统可以让流程自动化，但对接种数量和空间分布缺乏精确控制，这直接损害高通量试验的可重复性。 **工程化的解法是用微流控设备把类器官封在小体积基质中，精确控制形状、大小和细胞数：**
- 嵌在多孔板中的微工程水凝胶膜可在**同一焦平面**上同时生成数千个均一类器官，从而对数千个受药的结直肠癌类器官做实时自动成像
- 微流控高通量打印基质胶包埋的类器官液滴，既保证类器官间均一性，又保留组织异质性
- 液滴格式表面积体积比大，便于病毒感染和 T 细胞穿透，已用于 SARS-CoV-2 药物测试和快速 CAR-T 效力试验 **读出端则回到最朴素也最可靠的方案**——基于荧光或吸光度的细胞活力测定，再叠加高内涵成像与 AI 辅助分析。 **这里有一项我很看重的进展：组织微阵列可以把一整块 96 孔板的类器官嵌进一个 FFPE 蜡块。** 这把数字病理领域成熟的 AI 定量图像分析、免疫组化、多重免疫荧光、空间转录组乃至 MALDI 成像整套搬进了类器官研究。

## 【31:00-36:00】安全性与 ADME：能力清单，以及我必须承认的短板

# English Script: Human Organoids as 3D In Vitro Platforms for Drug Discovery
## 【0:00-04:00】Opening: a failure mode that will not go away
Good morning, everyone. Let me start with a fact that makes everyone in drug development uncomfortable. **Compounds that look excellent preclinically still fail at a high rate in humans.** And the failures cluster precisely where we have invested the most — liver toxicity, cardiac toxicity, CNS adverse effects. That pattern tells us the problem is not effort. The problem is the models. **For decades we have had two workhorses, each with one hard boundary.** **The first is the two-dimensional cell line.** Cheap, stable, universally available. But consider its provenance: cell lines have almost invariably undergone some form of malignant transformation — either they derive from tumours, or they have been extensively adapted to grow on plastic. **They do not represent tissue architecture, and they lack the complement of functional cell types of the organ they claim to model.** One observation captures this better than any argument: when transplanted, cell lines typically form tumours, whereas transplanted organoids produce healthy, functional tissue. **The second workhorse is the animal model.** It gives us what no dish can — the context of an entire organism, with systemic pharmacokinetics, inter-organ crosstalk and whole-body pathology. **But species differences are a hard boundary.** Many human diseases remain difficult to reproduce in animals, and physiological differences limit the accurate prediction of drug safety and efficacy. **Meanwhile the compliance landscape is shifting fast.** The US Congress passed the FDA Modernization Act 2.0 and then Act 3.0 to clarify the perceived requirement of animal models in drug development, and the FDA has announced its intention to phase out animal testing in the development of monoclonal antibody therapies and other drugs. Alternatives are no longer an academic preference; they are becoming a compliance requirement. **So the question becomes concrete:** is there a model that preserves human tissue architecture and cellular diversity, yet can be expanded, edited and screened like a cell line? **Let me state my position up front, so none of this sounds like advocacy.** Organoids are not a universal replacement; they are a highly reductionist platform. What I want to argue today is three things: where they are irreplaceable, how far short they fall on reproducibility and scale, and how far they still are from regulatory acceptance.
***
## 【04:00-09:00】Two routes, and the first strategic decision
**The definition is simple.** Organoids are 3D structures derived from stem cells that recapitulate key architectural and functional aspects of the corresponding tissue. But there are two very different roads to them, and they are frequently conflated. **The first road starts from pluripotent stem cells.** Its intellectual origin lies in the isolation of embryonic stem cells in the 1980s, and in Shinya Yamanaka's 2006 reprogramming of adult somatic cells into iPS cells using just four transcription factors — which largely resolved the ethical and sourcing problems of human ES cells. **The turning point came when the field moved from 2D culture to 3D aggregates.** Yoshiki Sasai's team found that pluripotent cells spontaneously self-organize into far more complex structures than anyone expected, and produced organoids resembling the retina and various parts of the central nervous system. **The strategic essence of this route: you must recapitulate embryonic development in sequence** — first direct cells into a germ layer with appropriate niche factors, then into organ-specific cell types with tissue-specific developmental cues.
- Cost: time (weeks to months) and maturity — organoids commonly reach only late fetal stages
- Benefit: coverage of brain, retina, lung, kidney, liver and intestine, **especially tissues with no active stem cells at all** **The second road starts from tissue stem cells.** Virtually every organ replaces worn-out cells through its own tissue stem cells, with the likely exceptions of heart muscle, the kidney glomerulus and most of the CNS. Haematopoietic stem cells were first; epithelial tissue stem cells were later identified in skin, cornea and the intestinal lining. **In 2009 we demonstrated that a single tissue stem cell from the intestinal epithelium can create ever-expanding epithelial organoids containing all relevant intestinal cell types.** The strategic advantage is direct: because the cells come from a biopsy of the target organ, this route bypasses lineage specification entirely, requires only expansion factors, and collapses the timeline from months to days or weeks. **I want to emphasize this dichotomy, because it is the selection principle behind everything that follows:**
- Brain, retina, heart muscle — you must go pluripotent
- Digestive tract, airway, prostate, tumour — tissue stem cells are faster and more robust **Either way, disease mutations can be introduced by CRISPR, with one constraint.** Edited cells must be highly proliferative to grow out from a single cell, so editing is typically performed in iPS cells or tissue stem cells, before lineage specification and differentiation — not on already-differentiated organoids. **Against the traditional models, the trade-offs are clear.** Organoids beat cell lines on physiological relevance and beat animals on throughput and cost; they lose to cell lines on throughput and to animals on systemic context. **And on regulatory acceptance — this is the crux — they are only "emerging and increasingly recognized," whereas animal models are long established.** That asymmetry is the tension running through this entire talk.
***
## 【09:00-15:00】The disease-modelling panorama: the criterion matters more than the catalogue
**Organoids have been applied to genetic disorders, metabolic disease, chronic inflammatory disease, infection and cancer.** I am not going to walk you through a catalogue. I want to give you a criterion. **When should you put the mouse down and reach for a human organoid? When the disease mechanism is itself human-specific.** Follow this chain of evidence, which recurs throughout the review:
- **Cystic fibrosis** — mice carrying _Cftr_ mutations, including ΔF508, do not develop the severe airway disease seen in patients: no mucus accumulation, no airway obstruction, no chronic lung infection
- **Parkinson disease** — human midbrain organoids with a loss-of-function _DNAJC6_ mutation show impaired dopamine neuron development, pathological α-synuclein aggregation, increased intrinsic firing frequency, and mitochondrial and lysosomal dysfunction via impaired WNT–LMX1A signalling; _Dnajc6_-knockout mice do not recapitulate the phenotype
- **Coeliac disease** — depends on specific HLA-DQ haplotypes mice do not possess; even humanized transgenic mice do not fully replicate the immune response or pathology
- **Human norovirus** — the leading cause of acute gastroenteritis worldwide; cell lines fail to support replication and mice are naturally resistant owing to receptor and immune differences; human intestinal organoids provided the first in vitro platform and identified enterocytes as the site of replication
- **Zika virus** — NS5 specifically targets human, but not mouse, STAT2 to inhibit type I interferon signalling **Do you see the pattern?** Five diseases, five organs, one argumentative structure. The organoid niche is not "better than the mouse" — it is that for human-specific mechanisms, the mouse has no answer at all. **Let me give the example I find most persuasive, because the clinic validated it in reverse.** Hydroxychloroquine, a blocker of endocytosis, was proposed for COVID-19 on the basis of cell line studies and was widely prescribed. **In organoid models, it did not inhibit viral reproduction.** Follow-up work with a CRISPR-engineered organoid biobank showed that SARS-CoV-2, MERS-CoV and SARS-CoV all specifically use TMPRSS2, which proteolytically activates the spike protein and allows direct fusion of the viral envelope with the host membrane — these viruses do not require endocytosis at all. **Clinical observation ultimately confirmed that hydroxychloroquine is ineffective against SARS-CoV-2.** The weight of this case: the organoid answer matched the clinic while the cell line answer misled an entire field — and along the way it nominated TMPRSS2 as an attractive pan-coronavirus target. **The cancer logic is equally clean.** Pancreatic, liver and breast cancers do not develop spontaneously in animals; experimentally induced tumours take considerable time and still fail to capture the complexity, genetic diversity and immune environment of human cancers. **Patient-derived tumour organoids can be established from virtually all carcinomas** using biopsy or resection material, are scalable, and closely recapitulate the histological, genetic and transcriptomic features of the original tumours. **One practical detail deserves attention.** Organoids carrying _KRAS_<sup>G12V</sup> grow without epidermal growth factor, which healthy organoids require — commonly used to distinguish tumour-derived outgrowth from normal tissue growth. That is not a trick; it is biological logic replacing physical purification.
***
## 【15:00-21:00】Deep dive I: cystic fibrosis — turning channel function into a measurable volume
**Now let me slow down and take one case all the way through**, because it is the only disease that has travelled the full distance from modelling to prediction to individualized treatment. **Cystic fibrosis is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by loss-of-function mutations in _CFTR_.** The gene encodes a chloride/bicarbonate channel crucial for ion and fluid homeostasis in multiple epithelial tissues, including lung and intestine. **Several CFTR modulators exist** — for example Orkambi, combining ivacaftor and lumacaftor, developed specifically for patients homozygous for Δ508, an estimated half of the CF population. **And here the central problem of precision medicine appears: patients carrying the same mutation respond very differently to the same drug**, because modulator activity is influenced by cellular and genetic background. The mouse cannot help, for the reason I gave earlier. **So how do you measure, in a dish, whether this patient's channel is open? Consider the design.** CFTR is activated by cAMP; forskolin raises cAMP by stimulating adenylate cyclase to convert ATP into cAMP. **Add forskolin to healthy gut or airway organoids, and CFTR-mediated water influx into the lumen triggers rapid swelling.** Organoids from patients with CF fail to swell. **Three pieces of elegance:**
- It translates a **molecular** channel function into a **geometric** change in volume — no electrophysiology, no labelling; a microscope suffices
- The readout is **continuous and quantitative**, so it captures partial restoration rather than a binary answer
- Precisely because it is simple, it is naturally suited to **high-throughput screening** **Then the decisive logical step: swelling failure can be rescued two ways.** By CRISPR-based correction of the _CFTR_ mutation — adenine base editors have corrected nonsense mutations in a CF organoid biobank — or by treatment with effective CFTR modulators. **But the step that matters most is external validation.** Drug responses measured in the swelling assay correlate well with clinical outcomes, including percentage of predicted forced expiratory volume in one second and sputum cell count. **Feel the weight of that.** The claim is no longer "my model resembles the disease," but "my model predicts whether this person will benefit from this drug." On that basis rectal organoids enabled personalized treatment, and cystic fibrosis became the first disease in which patient-derived organoids enabled a personalized medicine approach. **The strategy then spread to neighbouring airway genetics, with identical readout logic — find a functional phenotype you can see directly:**
- **Primary ciliary dyskinesia**, caused by mutations in _DNAH5/11_, _DNAI1_ and _CCDC39/40_, is studied in nasal airway organoids that allow direct visualization of beating cilia and of structural changes by electron microscopy; correction of _DNAH11_ by prime editing demonstrated gene therapy potential
- **SFTPB deficiency** — iPS cell-derived lung organoids recapitulated the characteristic abnormal lamellar-body formation, rescued by re-expressing wild-type _SFTPB_

## 【21:00-25:00】Deep dive II: MCLA-158 — can organoids actually produce a drug?
**By now someone is thinking the sharpest question: these are all models — has a real drug ever come out of them?** Yes. And the entire programme was run on organoids. **MCLA-158, petosemtamab, is a bispecific antibody** with one arm directed at EGFR family members and the other at the stem cell marker LGR5. **The entire discovery programme used patient-derived colon cancer organoid lines:**
- A library of more than 500 bispecific antibodies was generated
- Unbiased functional screening for growth inhibition and morphological alterations in a small panel of colorectal cancer organoid lines identified MCLA-158
- The candidate was then characterized in a broader set of healthy and CRC organoids **One decision inside the screen is the heart of the case: matched healthy colon organoids ran alongside the tumour organoids from the very beginning.** MCLA-158 eliminated CRC organoids but not healthy colon organoids. **In other words, the therapeutic window was not discovered later in safety studies — it was written into the hit criterion itself.** That is fundamentally different from finding maximal killing in cell lines and retrofitting selectivity afterwards. **The downstream results supported that choice.** MCLA-158 has shown KRAS-mutant CRC growth inhibition, blockade of metastasis initiation and suppression of tumour outgrowth in preclinical models of several epithelial cancer types — and moved from discovery to phase I in 30 months. **Ongoing clinical studies include:**
- Phase III registrational trials in first-line head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, as monotherapy and with pembrolizumab
- A phase II open-label trial in late-line metastatic CRC begun in late 2024 **More broadly, patient-derived tumour organoids and matched healthy counterparts have been extensively banked across tumour types**, reflecting inter- and intra-tumour heterogeneity, enabling study of tumour evolution, response variability, resistance mechanisms and novel targets. **Importantly, they display sensitivity to antitumour therapies comparable to their parental tumour tissues**, and correlations with clinical outcomes have been reported in pancreatic, gastrointestinal and breast cancers. **But I must state the boundary just as clearly:**
- **Outside oncology, translational evidence remains scarce and controlled clinical validation is largely lacking**
- **Negative predictive data are seldom reported**, which limits our understanding of where organoid-based prediction stops working
- Multiple randomized trials are ongoing to assess predictive value, particularly in radiotherapy and chemotherapy settings — their readouts will set the credibility ceiling for this field

## 【25:00-31:00】Deep dive III: platform engineering — why we flip, flatten and plumb our organoids
**My third case is not a disease but a class of engineering decisions**, and I will use it to answer the question of scale and of toxicity detection. **Start with a physical obstacle that is easy to overlook.** Conventional organoids are embedded in Matrigel — a solubilized basement membrane extract from a mouse sarcoma, rich in laminin 111, collagen IV and fibronectin, that supports polarity and survival. **The problem: these organoids expose their basolateral surface outward and keep the apical surface facing the lumen, while pathogen receptors, transporters and channels are asymmetrically distributed apically.** To reach the true target surface you must microinject into the lumen. **The field answered with three solutions, each an explicit trade-off:**
- **Flip it** — remove the Matrigel to generate apical-out organoids, mitigating delivery and diffusion while preserving 3D architecture
- **Flatten it** — the cell mass is still generated in 3D, then plated on a transwell where apical and basolateral surfaces are both accessible; you sacrifice three-dimensionality and gain reduced inter-well variability, faster high-content imaging, and non-invasive dynamic barrier readouts by trans-epithelial electrical resistance (image-based dye-diffusion assays serve as an alternative)
- **Plumb it** — bioengineered microfluidic platforms control delivery from both apical and basolateral sides via multiple chambers with inlets and outlets **From our own recent work: a bacterial protein, _Yersinia_ invasin, can be coated on 2D transwells and fully replaces Matrigel.** That matters considerably for standardization. **Now the complexity axis. Most organoid models lack three crucial components: vasculature, immune cells and innervation.** Without blood vessels, nutrient and oxygen delivery is limited, affecting growth, maturation and function especially in larger organoids — and since most drugs are given intravenously, the absence of vascularization complicates accurate drug evaluation. **Three strategies are in play:**
- Co-culture with endothelial cells, via 3D bioprinting or micro-engineered assembloids
- Microfluidic channels for blood flow — organoid-on-a-chip
- Incorporation of immune populations including macrophages, dendritic cells and T cells **This engineering has already bought safety signals conventional models cannot produce. Two examples I consider decisive.** **First, an immune-competent, vascularized kidney organoid-on-a-chip perfused with peripheral blood mononuclear cells.** A T cell bispecific antibody targeting an HLA-A2-presented WT1 peptide selectively killed WT1-expressing cells, showing that the system evaluates chemical and immune-mediated toxicities in one dynamic microenvironment. **Second, in the gut, intestinal patient-derived organoids and tumouroids supplemented with immune cells revealed tissue-specific on-target, off-tumour toxicities of T cell bispecific antibodies**, capturing clinical adverse effects that conventional models lacking immune components do not predict; extending this, human intestinal immuno-organoids integrating epithelial organoids with autologous tissue-resident memory T cells were used to investigate TCB-induced intestinal inflammation observed in cancer patients. **Notice that the direction of the argument has changed.** Earlier I said organoids close a species gap. Here the claim is stronger: for biologics whose mechanism depends on human immune cells engaging human epithelium, there is no cross-reactive preclinical species at all. Organoids are not an alternative here — they are the only available tool. **Finally, scale. The conventional procedure embeds organoids in matrix and seeds them into multiwell plates.** Robotics can automate it, but the approach lacks precise control over the quantity and spatial distribution of seeded organoids, which limits HTS reproducibility. **The engineered answer is microfluidic seeding in small ECM volumes with precise control of shape, size and cell number:**
- Micro-engineered hydrogel films in multiwell plates generate thousands of uniform organoids trapped in microcavity arrays **on the same focal plane**, enabling real-time automated imaging of thousands of drug-treated CRC organoids
- High-throughput microfluidic printing of Matrigel-embedded droplets ensures inter-organoid homogeneity while retaining tissue heterogeneity
- The droplet format's large surface-to-volume ratio enables efficient virus infection and T cell penetration, applied to SARS-CoV-2 drug testing and rapid CAR-T potency assays **On the readout side we return to the simplest and most reliable approaches** — fluorescence and absorbance-based viability assays — layered with high-content imaging and AI-assisted analysis. **One advance I rate highly: tissue microarrays allow a full 96-well plate of organoids to be embedded in a single FFPE block.** That imports the mature digital-pathology toolkit — AI-supported quantitative image analysis, immunohistochemistry, multiplex immunofluorescence, spatial transcriptomics, MALDI imaging — into organoid research.

## 【31:00-36:00】Safety and ADME: the capability list, and the shortfalls I must concede
**Put those three cases together and you get the current state of organoid safety assessment.** Let me scan organ by organ, focusing on what organoids add beyond existing methods. **Liver — hepatotoxicity is a leading cause of drug attrition, market withdrawal and restricted use after approval.**
- Functionally mature human liver organoids recapitulate the known hepatotoxicity of troglitazone, paracetamol, trovafloxacin and levofloxacin **with higher sensitivity than 2D hepatocytes** at clinically relevant concentrations
- Tissue stem cell-derived organoids **outperform HepG2 cells** in modelling drug-induced phospholipidosis, demonstrating capacity to evaluate metabolite-mediated toxicity
- PS cell-derived organoids established a high-throughput assay that **annotated the DILI potential of 238 drugs**, and modelled **genotype-specific susceptibility to bosentan**-induced cholestatic injury across donors
- Intrahepatic cholangiocyte organoids modelled chlorpromazine-induced bile duct injury; liver organoids were adapted to assess AAV vector performance and on-target hepatotoxicity in gene therapy **Kidney — PS cell-derived kidney organoids contain nephrons, collecting ducts, renal interstitium and endothelial cells**, self-organizing into podocytes, proximal tubules, loops of Henle and distal tubules.
- Differential apoptosis to cisplatin and gentamicin, with dose-dependent upregulation of KIM1
- Bioprinted organoids reproduce doxorubicin and aminoglycoside toxicity with high reproducibility
- But to be honest: they **still lack several key transporters and tubular filtration** **Gut — here is the most striking quantitative result in the field.** A human ileal tissue stem cell-derived organoid assay, validated against 31 reference drugs, predicts the clinical incidence of drug-induced diarrhoea with 90% accuracy. **Comparing human, rat and dog organoids also revealed species-specific sensitivity differences aligning with preclinical findings.** That matters enormously: organoids can not only replace animals but explain why animals mislead us. **Central nervous system — CNS adverse effects reportedly account for 7–34% of early- and late-stage project terminations.**
- Cerebral organoids revealed neurotoxic effects of vincristine on brain development, implicating ECM-mediated signalling and matrix metalloproteinases
- Mature cerebral organoids revealed dose-dependent damage from tranylcypromine via transcriptional suppression of BHC110/LSD1-targeted genes and histone H3 lysine 4 demethylation
- Paclitaxel-associated neurotoxicity has also been assessed **Heart — cardiotoxicity remains a major contributor to development failures and post-marketing withdrawals.**
- Cardiac organoids reproduce doxorubicin toxicity: reduced viability, elevated LDH and BNP, apoptosis, fibrosis and mitochondrial dysfunction
- Across FDA-recalled compounds (astemizole, cisapride, mibefradil and others), organoids detect dose-dependent ATP depletion and beat disruption **more effectively than 2D monolayers**
- The clearest demonstration of systemic value comes from multi-organ platforms: in liver–heart co-culture, hepatic conversion of clomipramine into desmethylclomipramine by cytochrome P450 reduces beating, disrupts calcium flux and kills cardiac cells — **effects absent when liver organoids are absent**; a six-tissue platform likewise showed hepatic metabolism of capecitabine and ifosfamide inducing downstream cardiotoxicity **All of this points one way: inter-organ crosstalk is itself part of the toxicity mechanism.** **Now the shortfalls, unvarnished. Application in absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion remains limited, for three reasons:**
- Liver and kidney organoid differentiation lacks maturity
- Small pharmacokinetic studies in animals are **simply so easy** that, despite translatability caveats, convenience often outweighs the perceived benefit of organoid predictions
- Addressing ADME demands enzyme activity representative of the in vivo situation, barrier functions recapitulating human physiology, and **sufficient tissue mass — millions of cells — to catalyse biotransformation** **Progress is real but partial:**
- A tissue stem cell-derived liver organoid system expresses broad phase I enzymes (CYP3A4, CYP2D6), phase II enzymes (UDP-glucuronosyltransferases, sulfotransferases) and transporters (OATP1B3, ABCB1/MDR1, MRP3), and was integrated with a millifluidic chip to study midazolam metabolism and bioavailability
- Monolayers of polarized human duodenal organoids discriminated high- from low-permeability compounds, showing functional MDR1 and BCRP activity
- Inside-out intestinal organoids show functional induction of CYP3A4 and MDR1 by rifampicin and calcitriol, and inhibition by verapamil and ivacaftor
- An organoid-derived proximal tubule epithelial cell-on-a-chip showed marked upregulation of OCT2, OAT1 and OAT3, with better drug uptake than chips using immortalized cells

## 【36:00-40:00】Regulation, boundaries, and where this lands
**Four minutes left, and this is the real destination of the talk.** **The main barrier to organoids entering drug development is not biology; it is qualification.** The challenges are model qualification, reproducibility, clinical translation, cost–benefit optimization and regulatory acceptance. **The reality: regulatory packages currently favour in vitro tools with low complexity, high throughput and low cost.** Companies do use organoids for exploratory and mechanistic studies in selected contexts of use, but those studies are rarely pivotal in determining safety for clinical trials. **The only way to change that is to demonstrate clear advantages over conventional methods — superior predictive power in specific contexts of use.** **The regulatory door is genuinely opening. In the regulatory context these platforms are defined as new approach methodologies, NAMs:**
- The FDA Modernization Acts 2.0 and 3.0 require the FDA to establish a pathway by which entities may apply to have non-clinical testing methods approved for a particular context of use
- The FDA's Alternative Methods Program pursues three aims: expanding qualification processes, providing clear guidance to external developers, and filling information gaps with applied research
- The OECD provides three key guidance documents: Good In Vitro Method Practices, guidance for describing non-guideline in vitro test methods, and guidance on validation and international acceptance of new or updated test methods for hazard assessment
- The EMA's voluntary submission of data procedure — the safe harbour approach — allows data to be generated, compiled and reviewed to help define a context of use for a NAM **One conceptual distinction worth remembering: characterization addresses biological relevance and scientific validity; validation establishes the reliability and relevance of a particular assay for a defined context of use.** **Now the qualifications that should govern how you evaluate this field:**
- **Most studies presented here are preclinical proof-of-concept work**, and validation against in vivo and patient data is still limited
- Establishing organoids as translational standards requires **additional forward and reverse translation studies**
- In neurological and other complex systemic diseases, organoids are further challenged by **the difficulty of modelling multicellular interactions and systemic pharmacokinetics**, constraining predictive utility **So my conclusion is not that organoids will replace animals, but something more conservative and more useful: this is a reallocation of questions.**
- Human-specific mechanisms, human cell–cell interactions, or decisions about one specific patient — send those to human organoids
- Whole-organism context and systemic pharmacokinetics — those still need in vivo work for now **Progressively incorporating organoids as a NAM to replace, reduce and refine animal models does offer real potential — but it should not compromise the established standards required for the assessment of medicines.** The equilibrium is to embrace these innovative methodologies while upholding the rigorous standards of the regulatory network. **Looking forward, three things must advance together:**
- Innovations in vascularization, immune system integration and multi-organ modelling
- Continued development and validation of standardized, automated platforms, with clear regulatory frameworks that facilitate industry adoption
- Enabling analytics: single-cell omics, high-speed image acquisition, AI-supported data analysis **My own expectation is that if those arrive, organoid-based platforms will become an integral part of the drug discovery pipeline, bridging basic science and clinical outcomes.** (A reasonable extrapolation from the outlook in the paper, not an established fact.) **Thank you. In the discussion, there is one question I would especially like to put to you: in your own disease area, is there a specific mechanistic question that a mouse cannot answer in principle?** That is exactly where an organoid belongs.
