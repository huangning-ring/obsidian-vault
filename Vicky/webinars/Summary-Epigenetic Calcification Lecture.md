# About the Lecturer
This lecture was delivered by **Tamás Arányi**, Professor at the HUN-REN Research Centre for Natural Sciences (Institute of Molecular Life Sciences) and the Department of Molecular Biology at Semmelweis University, Budapest, Hungary. Arányi has devoted over two decades to researching pseudoxanthoma elasticum (PXE), ectopic calcification, and the epigenetic regulation of gene expression.

# Opening
The author has been researching PXE for 20 years.
PXE was by the lack of ABCC6 which almost only express in liver cells. However, the feature -- ectopic mineralization seems unreasonable at all. Why does it happen?
Why is a gene that only express in liver can cause mineralization in other tissues? We tested its transcripting level by NGS, resulting that several kinase cascade pathway can be focused on inhibition of ABCC6.
Until 2014, the researchers found that ABCC6 can transport ATP to blood circulation, which soon be cut by ENPP1, generating PPi and AMP. We all know that PPi is a strong and effective inhibitor of calcification, so its deficiency must cause ectopic mineralization. That's the mechanism! This is the first leap in Tamás Arányi's research.
We all know that the ateral wall are constructed by 3 layers, and the calcification usually takes place in endometrium and mesentery.
Endometrium calcification is the marker of atherosclerosis while Mesentery calcification is driven by osteoblast transformation of AMCs. This is a positively regulared biology program. However, the above molecular mechanism was an important, single-factor depletion model driven by systemic PPi depletion.

# Epigenetics
## Relation to vascular calcification
The first connetion between epigenetics and vascular calcification is the phenomenon we mentioned above: transdifferenciation of VSMC is driven by essential transformation in cell destiny. Secondly, we have discussed environmental triggering factors of vascular calcification: oxidative stress and  phosphate elevation, which are normal stimulation factors to drive epigenetic changes.
However, there still keeps a limited number of reports to actually relate epigenetics to calcification.
## Specific epigenetic mechanism in vascular calcification
SIRT6 and HDAC9 both suppress RUNX2. When phosphate rises, they get blocked, and RUNX2 drives calcification. But since they target RUNX2 directly, not histones, this isn't truly epigenetic.
The real epigenetic proof: blocking HDACs in VSMCs increases calcification and RUNX2 expression. Knocking out HDAC1 — which targets histone H3 — gives the same result. That's true chromatin-level control.
Globally, as calcification gets worse, H3K9me3 drops and DNA becomes hypomethylated. The whole epigenome gets unstable.
## Epigenetic Clock
Just 350 CpG sites can predict your biological age. If your epigenetic age runs ahead of your real age, your cardiovascular death risk goes up.
## GWAS and Endothelin-1
A variant linked to coronary artery disease sits inside an aorta-specific enhancer. It controls endothelin-1, 600 kb away. Higher endothelin-1 protects against hypertension but increases coronary artery disease risk — all because of where this enhancer is active.
